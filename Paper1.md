## Abstract

**What kind of paper this is.** This is a *conceptual* contribution, and the label is a commitment rather than modesty: what the formal apparatus licenses is **sorting** (which cell a candidate account of transformation occupies) and **costing** (the agency price of a given degree of exogenous forcing), plus one conditional dynamical result. It licenses no claim that any transformation described here occurs, and the impossibility argued here — which is why the title says *limits* — is doubly conditioned — on a definition of control, and on identity contracts that factor through a bounded reading (see the scope box below, which sums every concession in one place). A reader looking for an empirical discovery will not find one, and should not read the formalism as supplying it.

This paper makes a single negative claim and defends it as severely as it can: given the definition of control stated below, the inherited apparatus of cybernetics and optimal control cannot describe existential transformation — metanoia, conversion, sanctification — without annihilating the agency of the subject it claims to transform. The argument is transcendental rather than constructive. It does not propose a new mechanism of transformation; it argues that the dominant family of mechanisms cannot so much as formulate the question.

The paper proceeds in three movements. First, it reconstructs the conditions any account of persistence under transformation must satisfy, using a diagnostic taxonomy (R/P/M/Lambda) that names the failure modes of persistence — dissolution, rigid repetition, and motion without retention — against aperiodic memory-preserving continuity. The identity-relevant functional *I* is presented not as a physical property discoverable in a dynamical flow but strictly as a *pragmatic-indexing metamodel*: a consistency contract that binds whoever claims that an identity persisted to a determinate set of geometric constraints.

Second, and centrally, the paper argues for the **Trilemma of Cybernetic Impossibility**. Control-theoretic accounts of conversion face three failures, jointly exhaustive given that definition of control. The Paradox of Endogenous Tautology shows that change generated by internal rules alone (Ashby's law of requisite variety and ultrastability) is the execution of a latent subroutine, not conversion: the system changes state precisely in order not to change organization. The Paradox of Exogenous Annihilation shows that coercive trajectory-tracking control forces the agency eigenvalues to negative infinity and zeroes adaptive diffusion: to hold a system on a prescribed trajectory by penalizing deviation, the controller must, in the perfect-tracking limit, kill the agent — finite-gain forcing instead pays a graded, monotone agency cost (whereas a mere trigger that releases a transition toward a latent attractor is a distinct, non-excluded case). The Paradox of Incommensurability argues that, on the realist reading of conversion the phenomenology commits to, optimization algorithms collapse at axiological discontinuities where the cost function itself is replaced, generating singularities and indeterminacy in backward induction (Bellman collapse). The space these three paradoxes leave empty is exactly the space a non-coercive transformation would have to occupy.

Third, the paper identifies the residual admissibility profile — Class G — that survives all three exclusions, distinguishes it categorically from a unilateral integrity-bounded transition (the U-limit) by their divergent post-jump topology rather than by adjectival shielding. The theological applications of this profile — to grace, sanctity, acedia and resurrection — are developed in the companion theological paper (in preparation), not here. Class G's ten-condition profile is shown to be *satisfiable* — an explicit system meets all ten simultaneously — and *discriminating*, in that each near-miss the profile is meant to exclude fails a condition it is predicted to fail; the conjunction is therefore neither empty by over-specification nor vacuous by under-specification. Its conditions are not, however, ten independent hurdles: six can be violated in isolation and the rest are entailed by others, so the filter's effective dimensionality is about six and the count of ten overstates its independent content.

The argument is positioned against the literatures it bears on. Against **Callard**'s proleptic rationality the thesis is narrowed rather than defended: the trilemma bites against *unanchored* value change, and a large class of ordinary value acquisition is proleptically anchored, so Callard is best read not as a rival but as a candidate occupant of the space the trilemma leaves empty. That concession is then subjected to its own non-vacuity check, since a narrowed thesis could be true and empty: because aspiration is on Callard's account *agential*, all non-agential value change lies outside it by construction, and the residual class is non-empty — change undergone rather than undertaken, non-deliberative moral change, and the conversion of the actively hostile — at the cost that the paper's domain is smaller than originally implied and is bounded by someone else's distinction. Against **Paul**'s transformative experience the obstacle is shown to be different in kind — hers epistemic and removable by information, this one structural and surviving complete information. **Pettigrew**'s aggregate-utility solution is read as the best worked-out instance of the reabsorption the trilemma predicts, with his strongest reply (that an aggregation rule is a constraint of rationality rather than a value) identified as the sharpest point of substantive disagreement. Restated without theological vocabulary, the trilemma is a claim about **corrigibility**: it predicts that corrigibility is unachievable by objective design, that value learning is reabsorption rather than genuine value change, and that the only admissible shape for externally occasioned transformation is external, receptivity-gated, and non-tracking.

The paper does not prove theology, the soul, or the existence of grace. It argues that classical control science lacks the tools even to ask whether such transformation is possible.

**Two delimitations of the impossibility claim, stated at the outset because the rest of the paper depends on them.** First, the result is *conditional on a definition of control*. The trilemma's exhaustiveness is not a discovery about the space of all conceivable algorithms; it follows from a substantive premise — that to be a control account *at all* is to define directed change relative to a held-fixed evaluative structure (a cost, a reward, an enumerated family of these, or a fixed higher-order objective). That premise is defended in section 7.5 — and for the most-pressing objection, the case of endogenous preference dynamics in which the agent revises its own value structure, it is sharpened from a defended premise into a theorem-shaped argument whose analytic inputs are standard results: a Meta-Optimization Collapse Theorem shows that an autonomous preference dynamics on a compact value space falls into a trichotomy (gradient descent on a meta-potential, unbounded dispersion, or bounded — asymptotically chain-recurrent — behaviour), none of which realizes conversion — by recurrence for states typical of an invariant measure, and, for the transients of dissipative dynamics, by the conceptual argument that settling onto an attractor already latent in the autonomous flow is not openness — so that conversion is *externally occasioned* (necessarily for measure-preserving value dynamics; for dissipative dynamics only on that interpretive reading, which is itself relative to where the observer draws the system boundary, §7.5) — and that external occasion is the excluded coercion horn only if it is deviation-penalizing tracking, the non-tracking trigger remaining the admissible Class-G profile. The premise remains contestable at its definitional root: a reader who holds that open-ended, novelty-driven, or value-base-mutating optimization is still "control" will read the result as a claim about a narrower family. The honest form of the thesis is therefore: *given that control is evaluation relative to a fixed evaluative point, conversion — the transformation of that point — cannot be formulated as control.* Second, the impossibility is *exact only in the high-dimensional, self-organizing regime*. As the companion paper concedes without hedging, a low-dimensional system whose identity/agency mode decomposition is supplied in advance by a designer is trackable by a multi-objective controller; the irreducibility argued here is a property of systems in which that decomposition must be carved out by the system's own viability-constrained dynamics, against a value structure genuinely novel rather than pre-enumerated. The title says "limits" rather than "impossibility" for this reason. The trilemma keeps its name, and "impossibility" in it is shorthand for this doubly-conditioned claim, used in that sense throughout.

**A note on terminology.** Several terms in this paper carry more rhetorical charge than their licensed content, and the reader should discount accordingly. "Impossibility" means the doubly-conditioned claim of the abstract (relative to a definition of control, in the high-dimensional regime), not absolute physical or logical impossibility. "Transcendental" marks the *form* of the argument (a claim about conditions of possibility of a kind of description), not a Kantian deduction. "Annihilation" is shorthand for the specific limit $D_{ag}\to 0$, not a metaphysical claim about destruction. "Category error" (used of the naive Mori–Zwanzig reading in the companion paper) and "non-holonomic constraint" (used of an eigenframe rotation there) are analogies that the companion paper itself later qualifies. Where these terms appear, they are load-bearing only in their defined senses; their ordinary connotations are not part of any claim.

## 1. General Introduction

A recurring problem across metaphysics, psychology, and theology is persistence under transformation: how can something remain recognizably itself while undergoing real change? Three errors frame the problem by negation. The first equates identity with perfect invariance — if something changes, it is no longer itself. The second equates identity with unrestricted flux — if anything can change, nothing constrains what persists. The third equates identity with copyable information — if the same pattern is reproduced, the same person continues. Each error is coherent in isolation; each fails on examination.

But naming the failure modes is only the diagnostic half of the problem. The constructive half — and the one that exposes a deep inadequacy in the dominant scientific vocabulary for directed change — concerns *transition*: granting that there is a target regime of healthy persistence, how does a subject move toward it from a dispersive or disordered regime? The natural answer, in an age whose default model of directed change is cybernetic, is *control*: identify the desired state, specify a cost or error signal, and drive the system toward it by feedback or forcing. This paper's central thesis is that this answer is not merely incomplete but *logically incompetent* for the specific case of existential transformation. The control paradigm cannot describe metanoia — conversion, the radical reordering of a person — without, in the very act of describing it, destroying the agency that makes the person a person rather than a mechanism.

This is a transcendental claim in the Kantian sense: it concerns the conditions of possibility of a certain kind of description. It does not say that conversion is impossible. It says that *the control-theoretic framework cannot formulate conversion as conversion*. The three available strategies — endogenous self-correction, exogenous forcing, and cost-function optimization — each collapse when applied to a subject whose identity-bearing trajectory and agentive openness must both survive the transformation. The collapses are not contingent engineering difficulties; they are structural, and section 7 argues that they are, given its definition of control.

The argument requires precise vocabulary, and the paper builds it in stages. Sections 3 through 6 develop the diagnostic taxonomy and the identity functional, with one crucial reframing relative to naive dynamical accounts: the functional *I* is not treated as a property one could in principle measure in a flow. It is treated as a *pragmatic-indexing metamodel*, a consistency contract. Section 7 states and argues for the Trilemma of Cybernetic Impossibility. Section 8 identifies Class G as the residual profile the trilemma leaves empty. Section 9 distinguishes Class G from a unilateral, integrity-bounded transition (the U-limit) by post-jump topology. Section 10 draws the conclusion. The theological applications are left to the companion theological paper (in preparation).

**The argument in condensed form.** *The problem.* A subject can change radically and remain itself, but only some changes preserve it. The question of transition is: by what kind of intervention can a subject be moved toward healthy persistence without being either left to its own devices or overpowered?

*The diagnostic scaffolding.* R names dissolution by disorder; P names rigidity by repetition; M names dispersal by motion without retention; Lambda names aperiodic, memory-preserving persistence. The taxonomy is purely diagnostic and indexed to a pragmatic contract *I*.

*The negative core.* The three control-theoretic strategies for moving a subject between regimes each fail. Endogenous change is tautological (Ashby): the system changes to stay organized. Exogenous control is annihilating: it kills agency to impose a trajectory. Optimization is incommensurable: it is undefined where the cost function itself changes. These are the three horns of the Trilemma of Cybernetic Impossibility.

*The residual.* Class G is the admissibility profile that survives all three exclusions — externally sourced (not endogenous), receptivity-mediated (not coercive), mode-selective (not totalizing), and aperiodic (not collapsing into optimization toward a fixed target). It is the shape of the space the trilemma leaves empty, stated as a type signature.

*The U-limit.* The U-limit and Class G are distinguished by post-jump topology, not by adjective. Theological applications of the residual profile are left to the companion theological paper (in preparation).

*The conclusion.* You are not, this paper argues, watching someone try to prove a miracle with equations. You are watching a proof that classical control science lacks the instruments even to state the problem.

## 2. Scope, Method, and Formal Status

This article belongs to the genre of programmatic critique in the philosophy of science. It does not present a complete mathematical classification, a finished metaphysics of the person, a proof of doctrine, or an empirical theory of consciousness. It should not be evaluated as a contribution to formal control theory in the engineering sense, nor as dogmatic theology. It offers a *transcendental negative result*: an argument that a dominant family of models cannot formulate a certain problem, together with the diagnostic vocabulary needed to see why.

The argument proceeds by diagnostic reconstruction and impossibility argument. First, it identifies the persistence-failure modes. Second, it fixes the identity functional as a contestable contract rather than a measured property. Third — the core — it argues that each control-theoretic strategy for transition fails when both identity and agency must be preserved. Fourth, it characterizes the residual admissibility profile. Fifth, it tests the negative grammar against theological stress cases.

The formal notation disciplines distinctions and states exclusions; it does not claim new theorems in dynamical systems. The framework operates at two levels, each independently evaluable; a third, theological level is developed in the companion theological paper (in preparation) and is not claimed here.

| Transcendental claim | The control-theoretic paradigm cannot formulate existential transformation without collapsing into tautology, annihilation, or incommensurable indeterminacy. | A positive mechanism of conversion is supplied. |

**Failure conditions of the critique.** The negative argument itself can fail, and naming how is part of its discipline. It would fail if: (i) some control-theoretic strategy could be exhibited that moves a subject toward Lambda-like persistence while provably preserving both $D_{id} < D_c$ and $D_{ag} > 0$ and surviving an axiological cost-function change — collapsing one of the three horns; (ii) the constitutive/indicative distinction collapsed under domain pressure, so that "preserving agency" had no determinate content; or (iii) the three horns turned out not to be jointly exhaustive, leaving a fourth control strategy unexamined. The critique earns its place by exposing these conditions rather than hiding them.

### 2.1 What a Negative Critique Claims and Does Not Claim

A transcendental negative critique establishes a limit, not a positive theory. The analogy is to impossibility results elsewhere in science: a proof that perpetual motion violates thermodynamics does not tell you how to build an engine, but it permanently rules out a class of attempts and redirects inquiry. This paper's result has the same structure. It does not tell anyone how conversion occurs. It rules out, with structural rather than empirical force, the attempt to model conversion as control. What remains after the ruling-out is not a mechanism but a *constraint on any adequate mechanism* — the admissibility profile of section 8.

One comparison must be disclaimed precisely because the previous paragraph invites it. Calling this an impossibility result "in the genre of the limitative theorems" is an analogy of *form*, not a claim of formal kinship, and the distinction is load-bearing. The structure at issue here — a system that must transform the very evaluative point relative to which its operations are defined — has a surface resemblance to the self-reference at work in the classical limitative results (Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, Tarski's undefinability of truth, Löb's theorem) and to the undecidability of non-trivial semantic properties of programs (Rice's theorem) and its descendants in the theory of self-modifying and successor-verifying agents. A recent result makes that neighbourhood concrete: Nayebi (2025) proves that deciding whether an *arbitrarily modified* agent will ever violate corrigibility is undecidable, by explicit reduction to the halting problem. That is a genuine limitative theorem about adjacent subject matter — but its object is a *decision predicate over programs* (does this modified program ever enter a violating state?), whereas the trichotomy of section 7.5 is a statement about the *dynamics of a fixed flow* on a compact value space; the two share a target of concern, not a proof, and the contrast if anything sharpens the disclaimer rather than softening it — it shows what a genuine undecidability claim in this area looks like, and this paper makes none. That resemblance is real and may be worth pursuing, but this paper does *not* establish, and does not assume, any formal reduction to those results: it specifies no provability predicate, fixes no formal system, and proves no undecidability. The trichotomy of section 7.5 is a dynamical-systems argument about autonomous flows on a compact value space, not a metamathematical one, and its force does not derive from any of the named theorems. Where the resemblance points — whether agency-preserving conversion is an instance, a generalization, or merely a structural echo of internal-successor-verification limits — is an open question for future work and is flagged as such, not imported as support. Readers should therefore treat the limitative-theorem comparison as motivational framing for the *kind* of claim being made (a permanent ruling-out rather than an empirical difficulty), and not as a borrowed proof; the argument stands or falls on section 7, independently of any connection to formal incompleteness or undecidability.

The critique is therefore valuable in proportion to the dominance of the paradigm it limits. Cybernetics, optimal control, reinforcement learning, and predictive-processing-as-control are the default scientific languages for directed change in the twenty-first century. To show that this entire family cannot so much as state the problem of non-coercive transformation is to clear the ground on which any positive account must be built.

This raises a fair objection about the status of the paper's mathematical notation, which should be answered before it accumulates. A reader may protest that the paper imports heavy notation — $\lambda_\parallel$, $\lambda_\perp$, $D_{ag}$, $D_{id}$, Koopman spectra — yet disclaims producing theorems, and that this is a convenient evasion: notation borrowed for authority while exempted from the rigor notation normally carries. The objection presupposes a false dichotomy — that mathematical writing is either engineering-style theorem-proving or else mere metaphor. The notation here does neither and claims neither. It functions as an *a priori syntactic limitation tool*: it specifies, in exact form, the boundary conditions any control model would have to satisfy to formulate agency-preserving transformation, and the argument then contends that those conditions cannot be jointly satisfied. The claim $\lambda_\parallel \to -\infty$ is an instance: it is not decoration and not a theorem of control engineering, but the precise specification of the asymptotic behavior brute exogenous control must impose to guarantee a trajectory — a condition stated sharply enough that its incompatibility with the agency condition $D_{ag} > 0$ can be exhibited rather than asserted. The top-level thesis is not a deductive proof and the paper apologizes for none: as §7.6 concedes, it is definitional. The one theorem-shaped result — the endogenous case of §7.5 — derives from standard dynamical theorems (Poincaré, Conley) in their almost-everywhere and ω-limit forms, and §7.6 states exactly what it adds. Elsewhere the formalism does the more limited work of fixing the conditions precisely and showing their co-satisfaction to be impossible. That is the whole of its mathematical ambition, and it is calibrated to exactly that.

### 2.2 The Identity Functional as a Pragmatic-Indexing Metamodel

The single most important methodological commitment of this paper is the demotion of the identity-relevant functional *I* from a putative physical property to a *pragmatic-indexing metamodel*. Naive dynamical accounts of identity are tempted to treat persistence as something one could read off a trajectory: inspect the flow, measure the correlations, and discover whether identity is preserved. This paper rejects that temptation explicitly, because it is the source of the circularity that makes such accounts vacuous.

On the present account, *I* is not detectable in the flow. It is a contract entered into by an observer — a clinician, an analyst, a theologian — who proposes to make a persistence claim. The contract has the following force: *if you assert that this system persisted as the same X, then you are committed to indexing the following geometric and dynamical constraints as the ones whose violation would refute your claim.* The constitutive/indicative partition is, on this reading, a heuristic-taxonomic choice of the observer, not a fact about the system. It says which features the observer's claim treats as load-bearing and which it treats as free to vary.

This demotion is not a weakness; it is the precondition for non-circularity. Because *I* is a contract fixed in advance rather than a property fitted after the fact, it can be violated — the observer can be shown to have committed to constraints the system fails to meet — and it can be contested by other observers who propose a rival contract. The thesis that identity questions are well-formed only relative to such a contract places the framework within the sortal-dependence tradition: "same?" is always shorthand for "same *what*?", and *I* formalizes the *what* (Wiggins 2001). The departure from a purely sortalist account is that *I* may index a practice or a memory-bearing process rather than a substance-kind, and that it carries a dynamical vocabulary (the correlation-power condition, the constitutive/indicative split) the bare sortal leaves unspecified.

The danger this guards against is circularity. If *I* were chosen after inspecting the trajectory, any pattern could be made persistent by selecting the contract that preserves it. The three locks of section 4.1 — domain justification prior to analysis, adversarial failure cases specified before classification, and independent contestability by experts not committed to the verdict — are what keep the contract honest. The claim "this system is Lambda-like" therefore never means "this system intrinsically instantiates Lambda." It means: relative to a pre-specified, externally contestable contract, the system retains the relevant correlation power over the declared timescale. Change the contract and the classification may change. This is not a defect; it is the epistemic discipline that makes a persistence claim falsifiable.

## 3. Identity as Trajectory, Not State

Throughout this article, remaining the same self does not mean retaining every property, memory, or psychological disposition. It means preserving a historically indexed *trajectory* through admissible change. A copied pattern, even if structurally identical, is not by that fact the same trajectory. This non-branching requirement is essential because fission, teletransportation, and uploading cases show that psychological or informational continuity can ramify (Parfit 1984; Lewis 1976; Williams 1970; Agar 2016).

A living organism persists while exchanging matter and energy. A person persists through memory revision, moral transformation, trauma, sleep, illness, and social re-description. The state of the system at any instant is not the carrier of its identity; the carrier is the continuity of the path, indexed by the contract *I*. Two consequences follow. First, identity is a four-dimensional notion: it concerns the shape of a history, not the contents of a moment. Second — and this is what the trilemma will exploit — any intervention that moves the system must be evaluated by what it does to the *trajectory and its openness*, not merely by what end-state it produces. A controller that reaches the target state by collapsing the path's future openness has not transformed the subject; it has replaced a trajectory with a terminus.

## 4. The Identity-Relevant Functional *I* as a Consistency Contract

The functional *I* is specified by a domain, an observable $f$, a tolerance $\epsilon$, and a timescale $\tau$. The quadruple $(I, f, \epsilon, \tau)$ is the content of the contract. It does not describe the flow; it commits the observer.

### 4.1 The Three Locks Against Circularity

A valid use of *I* requires three locks. First, **domain justification**: the contract must be motivated by the persistence question before any trajectory is inspected. Second, **adversarial failure cases**: the observer must specify, in advance, the transformations that would count as identity-destroying. Third, **independent contestability**: domain experts not committed to the target verdict must be able to challenge the contract. A contract that cannot be challenged is not a contract; it is a tautology.

### 4.2 Constitutive and Indicative Invariants as a Heuristic Partition

The contract partitions features into constitutive and indicative. A *constitutive* invariant is one whose destruction the contract treats as breaking the trajectory: its loss is replacement, not change. An *indicative* invariant tracks identity but may change without, on the contract, destroying the subject. The partition is heuristic and observer-relative: it expresses which features the persistence claim treats as load-bearing. It is also externally contestable — a critic can argue that a proposed constitutive invariant is in fact merely indicative, or vice versa. The partition does no work as a discovery about the system; it does all its work as a structuring of the claim.

### 4.3 Worked Applications and Adversarial Failure Cases

The discriminating power of *I* is shown by cases it handles and, crucially, by attempted contracts that *fail*.

**Moral conversion** preserves constitutive invariants — non-branching trajectory, responsibility-bearing history, practical agency — while reorganizing indicative ones — habits, desires, self-description. On a well-formed contract for personhood, this is transformation, not replacement.

**Teletransportation/fission** preserves copyable structure but breaks the non-branching trajectory. A contract for singular numerical personhood must, by its adversarial cases, classify this as identity failure despite pattern-continuity.

**A too-permissive contract.** Consider $I_{profile}$ = similarity of memory reports, personality profile, and behavioral dispositions. It tracks many features ordinarily used to recognize persons, and a broad tolerance permits learning and aging. Yet it fails in the fission case: if two successors each satisfy $d_{I_{profile}}(S, A) \le \epsilon$ and $d_{I_{profile}}(S, B) \le \epsilon$, then both would count as continuations, violating non-branching. The failure is not that $I_{profile}$ tracks irrelevant features; it tracks relevant *indicative* ones. Its error is mistaking indicative invariants for constitutive ones. The contract is rejected as too permissive.

**A too-restrictive contract.** Symmetrically, $I_{exact}$ = complete preservation of all memories, dispositions, and self-descriptions classifies moral conversion, trauma recovery, and ordinary maturation as identity-destruction. It treats indicative invariants as constitutive and is rejected as too restrictive.

These failures clarify the contract's role: it must be strict enough to reject branching copies and permissive enough to allow genuine transformation. A contract that cannot satisfy both under adversarial testing is not admissible for the domain. This is the substance of external contestability.

## 5. A Diagnostic Taxonomy of Persistence: R, P, M, and Lambda

The taxonomy classifies persistence-relevant behavior relative to a contract. It is not a set of mutually exclusive boxes for all systems; real systems combine noise, rhythm, chaos, and long-memory organization at different scales. A system behaves as R, P, M, or Lambda only relative to the question: which features must remain identifiable, over which timescale, under which transformations?

### 5.1 Class R: Random Disorder

Class R denotes regimes in which order is overwhelmed by stochastic disorder. Such systems may preserve transient structure but cannot reliably maintain identity-bearing memory. The failure mode is dissolution: the pattern is scrambled or erased. In theological analogy, R can describe dissolution into unstructured flux; the analogy is controlled, since R is a formal regime, not a moral category.

### 5.2 Class P: Mechanical Periodicity

Class P denotes strict periodicity, $x(t+T) = x(t)$, $T > 0$. It preserves structure by repetition, resisting dissolution by *closing* the space of transformation. Its entropy rate is zero in the strict case; it contains no new history. If identity were repetition, the perfect identity would be a loop. Living identity requires continuity through difference. In theological analogy, P corresponds to mechanical legalism or dead ritualization — order without responsiveness. The pathology is mechanical closure, where repetition replaces response.

### 5.3 Class M: Mixing-Chaotic Order

Class M is essential because not all failure is random or rigid. A Class M system is deterministic, bounded, and dynamic, with positive maximal Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_{max} > 0$ and decaying correlations $C_f(t) \sim e^{-\beta t}$, $\beta > 0$. Nearby trajectories separate exponentially and identity-relevant correlations vanish. This is motion without retention: energetic but dispersive. Waddington's epigenetic landscape makes the contrast vivid — a canalized trajectory persists through difference because the landscape retains its organizing valleys; Class M is that landscape flattened, in which the system still moves but no valley returns it to its course (Waddington 1957). Modern attention economies — algorithmic feeds, perpetual notification, rapid affective switching — illustrate the regime: they do not merely distract a pre-existing subject but may shape it into a pattern of motion without memory. In ascetic theology, this structure illuminates acedia, developed in the companion theological paper (in preparation).

### 5.4 Class Lambda: Aperiodic Memory-Preserving Persistence

Lambda is not a new mathematical class of dynamical systems. It is a persistence-relevant diagnostic regime: bounded, deterministic, non-mechanical, aperiodic, memory-preserving dynamics relative to a specified contract $(I, f, \epsilon, \tau)$. The same physical process may count as Lambda relative to one contract and as M relative to another; this observer-relativity is the correct epistemic structure for a persistence claim.

Formally, Lambda is picked out by a *correlation-power condition*. The identity-relevant observable must keep a non-vanishing share of its correlation with its own past over long times. In applications this is estimated over a window fixed in advance and checked against phase-randomized surrogates, so that an estimate indistinguishable from its surrogates does not license a Lambda attribution. Class M may be just as deterministic, bounded and non-periodic, but it loses that correlation, and Lambda retains it. The formal statement, with its spectral and information-theoretic readings, is in Appendix A.1.

Crucially, the correlation-power condition is *necessary but not sufficient*, and is not the locus of the distinction. Long-memory processes also retain correlation power. What separates Lambda from a long-memory process is the relation between the flow and the contract *I*: a long-memory process retains power on *some* observable, whereas Lambda requires retention on the *specific* observable the contract designates as carrying a constitutive invariant. The dynamical vocabulary fixes the genus; the contract fixes the species. A critic who shows two systems sharing every dynamical signature yet differing in contract-relevant invariant has not refuted Lambda; they have illustrated why the indexing to *I* is load-bearing and the dynamics alone are not.

| Related concept | Similarity to Lambda | Key difference |
|---|---|---|
| Quasi-periodicity | Structured aperiodicity with organized frequency content. | May not involve contract-relevant memory or the constitutive/indicative distinction. |
| Metastability | Temporary stability under perturbation. | May describe transient rather than persistent organization. |
| Attractor dynamics | Organized resistance to perturbation. | Can freeze agency by reducing the system to a low-dimensional basin. |
| Long-memory processes | Temporal retention and slow decay of correlations. | May retain statistically without preserving the invariant the contract makes constitutive. |

## 6. Persistence Is Not Stasis

Class P makes vivid why persistence and stasis must be distinguished. A periodic process preserves form by repeating it, but personal, moral, and spiritual persistence cannot be reduced to repetition. A person incapable of growth, repentance, or new understanding has not achieved perfected identity; such a person has entered mechanical closure.

The taxonomy R/P/M/Lambda now names the space of persistence-relevant regimes. R dissolves the subject. P freezes it. M disperses it through motion that cannot remember. Lambda names the regime in which the subject neither dissolves, freezes, nor forgets. But the taxonomy is *purely diagnostic*. It classifies what is happening to a system; it cannot say how a system moves from one class to another without losing what matters. A system in Class M does not spontaneously enter Lambda; a system in Class P does not spontaneously recover openness.

This is the threshold at which the central question of the paper appears — and at which, the paper will argue, the dominant scientific answer collapses. The intuitive answer to "how does a system move toward Lambda?" is *control*: specify the target, drive the system there. Section 7 argues that control, in each of its three forms, either is no transformation at all (it merely re-expresses the system's own dynamics), or achieves transition by destroying the agent, or cannot even be defined across the axiological discontinuity that conversion essentially involves.

## 7. The Trilemma of Cybernetic Impossibility

### 7.1 The Central Problem: Why Control Theory Cannot Formulate Conversion

The diagnostic taxonomy poses a transition problem: move a subject from a dispersive regime (M), a rigid regime (P), or a disordered regime (R) toward Lambda-like persistence. The default scientific framework for directed transition is control theory in its broad sense — cybernetics, optimal control, dynamic programming, and their descendants. This section argues that this framework cannot formulate the transition that conversion names, because conversion is defined by two constraints the framework cannot jointly honor: the identity-bearing trajectory must be preserved ($D_{id} < D_c$) *and* the agency-bearing openness must be preserved ($D_{ag} > 0$), across a transformation that genuinely reorders the subject rather than merely relocating it within a fixed value structure.

The framework offers exactly three strategies for producing transition. The change can be generated from within the system's own dynamics (endogenous control / feedback). It can be imposed from outside by force on the state (exogenous control / forcing). Or it can be computed as the solution to an optimization over a value or cost function (optimal control / dynamic programming). These three exhaust the directed-change repertoire of the cybernetic tradition, from its foundational statement (Wiener 1948) through its modern reinforcement-learning descendants (Sutton and Barto 2018). The trilemma shows that each of the three fails, and that the three are jointly exhaustive of the control paradigm. What remains — the conjunction of properties no control strategy can deliver — is the admissibility profile of section 8.

The two constraints need a working vocabulary. Near the region where the subject's identity is preserved, its possible changes split into two kinds of direction:
- *identity-bearing* directions, along which uncontrolled spread destroys identity;
- *agency-bearing* directions, along which variation *is* the subject's adaptive openness.

Conversion requires three things together. Deviation along the identity-bearing directions must contract. Openness along the agency-bearing directions must be *protected*: the rate at which agency-bearing excursions are pulled back, written $\lambda_\parallel$, must be held near zero, so that agency is neither frozen nor dispersed. And the two spreads must satisfy the constraints the paper writes as $D_{id} < D_c$ (identity-bearing spread below a critical value) and $D_{ag} > 0$ (agency-bearing openness preserved). The formal decomposition is in Appendix A.2. The trilemma is that no control strategy can establish this profile.

### 7.2 The Paradox of Endogenous Tautology (Ashby's Closure)

The first strategy generates the change from within: the system's own feedback reorganizes it toward the target. Call the internal vector field $F_0$, so that the transformative term lies in $\langle F_0 \rangle$, the span of the system's own dynamics.

The paradox is that endogenously generated change is not conversion but the execution of a latent subroutine. Ashby's analysis of ultrastable systems is decisive here. An ultrastable system is one equipped with a mechanism that changes its own parameters whenever an essential variable leaves its viable bounds, until the variable is returned to those bounds (Ashby 1960). The law of requisite variety states that such a system can absorb disturbance only to the extent that its regulator has at least as much variety as the disturbance (Ashby 1956). The combined upshot is structural: an ultrastable system *changes state precisely in order to preserve its organization*. The parameter change is in the service of homeostatic closure. Whatever new behavior appears was already a reachable configuration of the system's own variety — a latent subroutine, selected by the disturbance, deployed to restore the prior organization.

This is the formal negation of conversion. Conversion is the change of the organization itself — the reordering of the field of value, the replacement of one cost structure by another (section 7.4). Endogenous change cannot deliver this, because by construction it operates to *keep the organization fixed*. Formally: if the transformative field lies in $\langle F_0 \rangle$, then the "new" trajectory is a trajectory the system's own dynamics already admitted; the system has moved within its existing organization, not transcended it. The change of state masks the conservation of organization. In the theological register, this is the structure of self-salvation: the creature reorganizes itself, calls the result transformation, and has in fact only redeployed its own latent variety to preserve its prior closure. Endogenous control is therefore excluded not because it fails to produce change, but because the change it produces is, by Ashby's analysis, organization-preserving and hence not conversion.

### 7.3 The Paradox of Exogenous Annihilation (The Death of Agency)

The second strategy imposes the change from outside, by direct force on the state. The relevant case must be specified precisely, because "external input" is far too broad: what the second horn concerns is *coercive trajectory-tracking control* — a control architecture in which an input $u_t$ drives the system along a prescribed path and asymptotically penalizes deviation from it, overriding the system's own dynamics wherever they would depart from the commanded route. It is this class, and only this class, that the paradox excludes. Inputs that merely trigger or release a transition — that push the system through a bifurcation toward an attractor already latent in its own dynamics, without thereafter penalizing departures from a commanded path — are a different category entirely and are treated separately below; they are not what the second horn rules out.

The paradox is that brute trajectory-tracking control achieves the target trajectory only by killing agency. Consider what it takes to *hold* a system on a prescribed trajectory by vector force in the presence of the system's own dynamics and noise. To make the imposed trajectory dominate, the controller must suppress the tangential degrees of freedom that would otherwise carry the system away from the prescribed path. In the linearized picture, this means driving the tangential (agency) eigenvalues strongly negative — in the limit of perfect tracking, $\lambda_\parallel \to -\infty$ — so that any agentive excursion is instantly contracted back onto the commanded trajectory. Equivalently, the agency-bearing diffusion is suppressed: $D_{ag} \to 0$. The commanded path is realized, but the agency-bearing directions that constitute the subject's openness, exploration, and capacity for self-originated variation has been collapsed. The annihilation is a property of *deviation-penalizing tracking* specifically: it is the penalty on departure from the commanded route that forces the tangential contraction.

This is annihilation in the only sense relevant to identity-bearing systems. The subject reaches the target *state*, but as a contracted point with no tangential freedom — a Class P closure imposed from outside. The trajectory has become a terminus; the agent has become a mechanism executing a command. The condition $D_{ag} > 0$ that conversion requires is precisely the condition trajectory-tracking control must violate to guarantee the commanded path. One cannot hold an agent on a prescribed trajectory by force and leave it free to depart from it; the freedom to depart is exactly what the tracking penalty must suppress. In the theological register, this is coercion: the creature is moved but does not participate, because participation *is* the tangential openness the controller had to kill. Trajectory-tracking control is therefore excluded not because it fails to produce the target state, but because producing it by deviation-penalizing force requires $D_{ag} \to 0$, which is the destruction of the agent.

One refinement keeps this horn from overclaiming. The collapse $D_{ag}\to 0$ is the *perfect-tracking limit* (zero asymptotic tracking error, infinite gain). Real forcing is finite-gain: a controller that holds the state within a bounded error tube — for instance funnel control or prescribed-performance control, which guarantee a prescribed error bound with *finite*, state-dependent gain (Ilchmann, Ryan, and Sangwin 2002; Bechlioulis and Rovithakis 2008) — leaves $\lambda_\parallel$ finite and $D_{ag}>0$. The honest statement of the horn is therefore graded, not binary: deviation-penalizing forcing reduces the agency-bearing diffusion *monotonically* with the tightness of the imposed bound, annihilating it only in the limit of exact tracking. "Annihilation" names that limit; for finite bounds the relevant quantity is the *agency cost* of forcing, which is strictly positive and increasing in the control authority exerted. The theological reading — coercion as the death of participation — is the limit point of a continuum of partial coercions, each of which suppresses, in proportion to its authority, exactly the tangential openness participation consists in. Nothing below the limit is full annihilation; everything below it still pays an agency cost, and that monotone cost, not a binary kill, is what the second horn properly asserts.

A critical qualification, which also forecloses an apparent conflict with the companion paper's biology, must be made explicit here. The annihilation result does *not* hold for exogenous *trigger* or *activation* inputs — external signals that push a system through a bifurcation toward a high-dimensional attractor already latent in its own dynamics, and then release. Such an input forces a transition but imposes no commanded path and penalizes no deviation, so it does not drive $\lambda_\parallel \to -\infty$ and does not collapse $H(\pi)$; on the contrary, it can *expand* the repertoire by moving the system out of a low-dimensional trapped regime into a richer attractor. The clearest biological instance is the ascending neuromodulatory reactivation of anesthetic emergence — the locus-coeruleus noradrenergic surge that lifts cortex out of the low-dimensional burst-suppression or delta regime and restores high-dimensional dynamics. That signal is exogenous, classical, and fully describable, and it *generates* rather than destroys agentive openness ($H(\pi)\uparrow$). It is no counterexample to the second horn, because it is not trajectory-tracking control: it triggers a transition toward a latent attractor rather than holding the system on a penalized path. The second horn excludes deviation-penalizing tracking, not exogenous causation as such — and the distinction between a trigger toward a latent attractor and a Class G reconfiguration of the generator (which opens an attractor the prior dynamics did *not* contain) is taken up in section 8.

### 7.4 The Paradox of Incommensurability (Bellman Collapse)

The third strategy computes the change as the solution to an optimization: define a cost or value function $J$, and let the optimal policy be the one that minimizes expected cost (or maximizes expected value) over the trajectory. This is the apparatus of optimal control and dynamic programming, and it is the most sophisticated of the three, because it appears to honor both constraints — it can preserve identity by penalizing destructive excursions and preserve agency by leaving the policy free within the value landscape.

The paradox is that optimization is *undefined* across the kind of transformation conversion essentially is. Conversion is not a movement to the optimum of a fixed value function. It is a change in *what counts as value* — a reordering of the cost function itself, $J \to J'$. The convert does not get better at satisfying their old preferences; their preferences are transformed. The thing that was salient as a good ceases to be a good, and what was invisible becomes the organizing end. This is an axiological discontinuity.

It is worth blocking a charge of circularity at exactly this point, since the whole trilemma turns on it. The objection would run: the paper has simply *defined* conversion as a change no fixed value structure can represent, and then triumphantly observed that value-structure-fixing methods cannot represent it — a definitional trick wearing the costume of a transcendental result. The charge is partly right, and §7.6 concedes that the top-level thesis is definitional. What it does not reach is the axiological discontinuity itself, which was not introduced to defeat Bellman: it is an independently attested datum that the analysis *encounters*, not one it manufactures. The radical reordering of what a person values — such that former goods lose their grip and a previously invisible end becomes organizing — is described, long before any control theory, in the moral-psychological and religious literatures on conversion, in the clinical phenomenology of value change after trauma and recovery, and in the philosophical literature on transformative experience, where the defining feature is precisely that the agent cannot evaluate the post-transformation state by their pre-transformation preferences (Paul 2014). The datum has content and standing on its own; the paper takes it as given and then *discovers* that this independently characterized phenomenon collapses the Bellman recursion. That order matters: the definition of conversion is fixed by the phenomenology, and the incompatibility with optimization is a consequence found downstream, not a premise smuggled upstream. A genuinely circular argument would have no work left to do once the definition was stated; here the work — showing that backward induction is ill-posed across $J \to J'$ — is a substantive step that could in principle have failed (it does not fail for *parametric* value change within a fixed family, which optimization handles fine; it fails only for the genuinely novel $J'$ the phenomenology describes). The distinction between those two cases is exactly what makes the result more than a tautology.

At such a discontinuity, the machinery of dynamic programming collapses. The Bellman equation defines the value of a state recursively, by backward induction from terminal costs through the value function: the optimal value at a state is the immediate cost plus the discounted optimal value of its successors (Bellman 1957). This recursion presupposes a *single, fixed* cost function across the horizon. If the cost function changes mid-trajectory — if at some time the relevant cost ceases to be $J$ and becomes $J'$ — then backward induction is ill-posed: the value of earlier states would have to be computed against a terminal structure that does not yet exist at those states and is incommensurable with the structure that did. There is no well-defined optimal policy across the discontinuity, because "optimal" has no fixed referent across it. Formally, the value function is singular at the transition and the optimal control is indeterminate: the optimization problem that dynamic programming solves is not merely hard but undefined when $J \to J'$.

This is not a numerical instability to be regularized away. It is a category failure. Optimization can describe *improvement within a value structure*; it cannot describe *the transformation of the value structure*, because the transformation is exactly the thing optimization holds fixed in order to be well-posed. Conversion, by definition, transforms the value structure. Hence optimal control cannot formulate conversion: it can model a saint getting better at being the person they already were, but not the becoming of a different person whose very metric of the good has changed. In the theological register, this is why grace cannot be modeled as the creature optimizing toward a goal: the goal — the entire order of desire — is what grace reorders. Optimization is therefore excluded not because the computation is intractable, but because the problem it would solve is undefined precisely at the axiological discontinuity conversion consists in.

**A deflationary alternative that must be confronted, not waved away.** The strongest reply to this entire horn is not that backward induction survives a genuine $J\to J'$, but that *there is no genuine $J\to J'$ to survive*. On this reading, what the phenomenology reports as a categorical reordering of value is in fact *parametric* change within a single, higher-dimensional value function $J^{*}$ that the agent could not previously represent — a value structure whose relevant coordinates were latent, not absent. The convert does not acquire a new metric; they acquire access to dimensions of a metric they always implicitly had, and the felt discontinuity is an artifact of limited introspective access to one's own latent value space, not a real singularity in the optimization. This alternative is simpler, it preserves the well-posedness of dynamic programming throughout, and it dissolves the Bellman horn without any appeal to incommensurability — so if it holds, the third horn fails. The paper cannot defeat it by stipulation, and the §7.4 distinction between novel and parametric $J'$ does not by itself exclude it, because the deflationist will simply classify *every* apparent novelty as latent-parametric after the fact. What the paper can do is locate the empirical and conceptual cost of the deflationary move and decline to pay it. Empirically, the latent-$J^{*}$ hypothesis requires that the post-conversion preferences be, in principle, *representable in coordinates the pre-conversion agent already possessed* — that the saint's new ordering be a projection of a single fixed $J^{*}$ onto a previously unattended subspace. The transformative-experience literature is precisely the claim that this fails: that the agent cannot, even in principle, evaluate the post-transformation state by any extension of the pre-transformation preferences, because the epistemic content needed to fix those coordinates is available only *from inside* the transformed state (Paul 2014). If that is right, $J^{*}$ is not a fixed function the agent partly accessed; it is a post-hoc reconstruction that smuggles the outcome into the premises. The disagreement is therefore not resolvable by the formalism — it turns on whether transformative experience is genuinely epistemically transformative or merely informationally incomplete — and the paper's commitment is explicit: the Bellman horn holds *if* axiological discontinuity is real, and reduces to the deflationary reading *if* it is not. This is a conditional, and it is stated as one. The horn is not a proof that the deflationist is wrong; it is the argument that *on the realist reading of conversion the phenomenology already commits to*, optimization is undefined — and that the deflationist escapes the horn only by denying that reading, at the cost of holding that no one ever undergoes a change of values they could not have anticipated, which is itself a strong and contestable empirical thesis.

### 7.5 The Joint Result: A Transcendental Limit

The three paradoxes are jointly exhaustive of the control paradigm. Any control-theoretic account of transition must generate the change endogenously, impose it exogenously, or compute it by optimization — there is no fourth source of directed change within the paradigm. And each fails on a constraint conversion requires:

| Control strategy | Mechanism | Failure | Constraint violated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endogenous (feedback / ultrastability) | Change generated by the system's own dynamics $F_0$ | Organization-preserving by Ashby; change of state masks conservation of organization | Not conversion: no change of the value structure |
| Exogenous (trajectory-tracking) | Prescribed path held by penalizing deviation | Requires $\lambda_\parallel \to -\infty$, $D_{ag} \to 0$ to guarantee the path | $D_{ag} > 0$ (agency destroyed) |
| Optimization (dynamic programming) | Policy minimizing a fixed cost $J$ | Bellman recursion undefined when $J \to J'$; value singular, policy indeterminate | Well-posedness fails at the axiological discontinuity |

The conjunction is the transcendental result. The control paradigm *cannot formulate* a transformation that (i) genuinely reorders the subject's value structure rather than redeploying its latent variety, (ii) preserves the subject's agency rather than collapsing it to execute a command, and (iii) is well-defined across the reordering rather than singular at it. But these three are exactly the marks of conversion. Therefore conversion is not a control problem that is merely hard; it is a transformation the control paradigm lacks the conceptual resources to state.

The negative force of this result is its whole point. It does not say what conversion is. It says, with structural rather than empirical certainty, what conversion is *not*: it is not endogenous self-optimization, not exogenous imposition, and not value-function optimization. The space of properties left over — externally sourced (escaping the endogenous horn), agency-preserving and non-coercive (escaping the exogenous horn), and operating *through a reordering of the value landscape itself* rather than optimization within it (escaping the Bellman horn) — is not a void. It is a precisely shaped admissibility profile. Section 8 gives it a name and a type signature.

**Does modern control escape the trilemma?** The three horns as stated above engage classical linear feedback, brute vector forcing, and deterministic dynamic programming, and a fair objection is that this is a caricature: contemporary control theory is far richer, and at least three modern families appear to evade the specific failures named. They must be engaged directly rather than left to the reader, because if any of them genuinely formulates conversion, the transcendental claim fails. The three are dual control, switched and hybrid systems, and adaptive or reinforcement-learning control. The claim defended here is not that these families are primitive, but that each, on inspection, falls under one of the three horns for a reason that is structural rather than incidental — and that the reason is always the same: each presupposes, in advance, the very value structure that conversion transforms.

*Dual control* (in Feldbaum's sense) gives the control input a double role: it probes, generating information that reduces uncertainty about the system, and it regulates, driving the state (Feldbaum 1965). This genuinely escapes the *static* reading of the endogenous horn, because a dual controller actively reshapes its own belief map rather than merely redeploying a fixed repertoire. But it does not escape the horn's substance. The probing-and-regulating is conducted in the service of a fixed higher-order objective — the dual controller optimizes expected cost under uncertainty, and the *cost functional that defines what counts as a good probe and a good regulation is given in advance and held fixed*. Dual control changes the agent's beliefs; it does not change what the agent values. It is therefore a sophisticated instance of the optimization horn, not an escape from it: the Bellman collapse recurs the moment the relevant cost ceases to be the dual controller's $J$ and becomes an incommensurable $J'$, because the exploration-exploitation tradeoff it computes is defined relative to $J$.

*Switched and hybrid systems* appear to dissolve the Bellman collapse outright: a transition between cost functionals $J \to J'$ is not a singularity for them but a well-documented structural bifurcation, handled by switching among a family of controllers or modes (Liberzon 2003). This is correct, and it is the most serious of the three objections — but it relocates the problem rather than solving it. A switched system handles the transition $J \to J'$ only when the *family* $\{J, J', \dots\}$ and the switching surfaces between its members are specified in advance. The switching logic presupposes that all the value structures the system might occupy are already enumerated and that the rule for moving among them is part of the design. Conversion is precisely the case this excludes: the arrival of a $J'$ that was *not* in the predefined family, governed by no antecedently specified switching surface, because the new order of value is not a selection from a menu but the appearance of a term the menu did not contain. A switched system can model a person changing which of several known goods they currently pursue; it cannot model a person coming to be governed by a good that was not in their space of options at all. Against a genuinely novel $J'$ the switched system is back in the Bellman horn, because it has no switching surface defined onto a mode it does not possess.

*Adaptive and reinforcement-learning control*, including multi-agent and real-time-identification schemes, learn and revise their policies online and so seem to need no fixed model. But learning a policy is not transforming a value structure. An RL agent revises its policy to better satisfy a reward signal that is *exogenously fixed*; the reward function is the agent's value structure, and standard RL holds it constant by construction. Where the reward itself is made to change — inverse RL, reward learning, meta-learning — the change is governed by a fixed higher-order objective (match observed behavior, maximize meta-reward), which simply moves the fixed value structure up one level and reproduces the optimization horn there. At whatever level the objective is ultimately fixed, the Bellman collapse applies to a $J'$ incommensurable with that level's objective; and if no level is ultimately fixed, the scheme has no defined notion of "better" and computes nothing. The regress does not rescue the paradigm; it shows that the paradigm always terminates in some fixed objective, and conversion is the transformation of whatever that terminal objective is.

The common structure is now visible and is the real content of the exhaustiveness claim. The control paradigm, in every variant, presupposes a fixed point of evaluation — a cost, a reward, a meta-objective, an enumerated family of these — relative to which "directed" change is defined. Dual control fixes the cost; switched systems fix the family and the switching surfaces; adaptive control fixes the terminal objective. Conversion is the transformation of that fixed point itself. This is why the trilemma is exhaustive not as a taxonomy of named techniques — which will keep multiplying — but as a statement about the *form* of any control account: to be a control account at all is to define directedness relative to a held-fixed evaluative structure, and conversion is exactly the change that structure cannot represent because it *is* that structure's transformation. The exhaustiveness is therefore not the claim that no future control technique exists; it is the claim that any technique recognizable as control inherits the fixed evaluative point, and inherits with it one of the three failures.

**The status of the exhaustiveness premise, stated honestly.** Everything in the preceding paragraph rests on one premise — *to be a control account at all is to define directedness relative to a held-fixed evaluative structure* — and that premise is not a theorem. It is a substantive philosophical commitment about what the word "control" picks out, and it must be flagged as such rather than allowed to pass as a discovery, because the entire transcendental force of the result is borrowed from it. Three families of recent work press on it directly and must be conceded their due. *Open-ended evolutionary and novelty-search algorithms* (Lehman and Stanley) deliberately abandon a fixed objective, rewarding divergence from past behavior rather than progress toward a goal; *formal theories of artificial curiosity and intrinsic motivation* (Schmidhuber; Singh, Barto, and Chentanez) make the reward signal a function of the agent's own changing model, so that what counts as valuable is itself revised by learning; and *endogenous-preference models* in economics (Bowles) treat the value structure as an output of the dynamics rather than a boundary condition on them. A reader committed to counting these as "control" will, correctly, read the trilemma as bounded to the narrower family that holds an evaluative point fixed, and will regard the present result as conditional on a definition they reject. This objection cannot be dissolved by argument; it can only be located. The paper's reply is not that these programs are not genuine — they are — but that each, examined at the level where "better" is ultimately defined, still terminates in *some* invariant criterion: novelty search fixes the *measure of novelty* and the *behavioral space* over which divergence is rewarded; curiosity-driven learning fixes the *meta-objective* (compression progress, prediction-error reduction) that makes one model-change preferable to another; endogenous-preference models fix the *higher-order dynamics* by which preferences update. The regress is real, and the paper's claim about it can be made precise and, for the endogenous case, stated as a theorem rather than asserted — a theorem for states typical of an invariant measure, with the dissipative case resting on interpretation (see the qualifications below). We state it as a theorem about the dynamics of the value state itself.

**The Meta-Optimization Collapse Theorem (endogenous-preference case), in outline.** Suppose the agent revises its own preferences by an autonomous dynamics of its value state, and suppose the value state stays within a bounded region, as it must if the agent is to persist rather than have its values diverge. Every such dynamics falls into exactly one of three cases, and none of the three is conversion:
- *Case 1, the integrable case (Bellman returns).* The revision is descent on a single higher-level potential. The "new" values are a moving coordinate within one fixed objective, and the Bellman horn of §7.4 applies to that objective exactly as it applied to a fixed cost.
- *Case 2, the dispersive case (Class M).* The value state wanders without settling. Preferences cycle, and there is no directed, permanent reordering.
- *Case 3, the recurrent case.* The value state settles onto a bounded attractor. What excludes conversion depends on the attractor:
  - at a fixed point, openness collapses;
  - on a limit cycle, the change is rigid repetition;
  - on a strange attractor, the system is open but keeps returning arbitrarily close to where it has been, which is the negation of permanent reorganization into a new regime.

The formal statement is in Appendix A.3. It includes the Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition that makes the three cases exhaustive, and the measure-theoretic form of the recurrence argument: recurrence holds with respect to the attractor's invariant measure, not the ambient one.

*The closure of the trichotomy.* Conversion requires *both* permanent directed reorganization into a new regime (escaping Cases 2 and 3) *and* preserved non-coercive aperiodic openness (escaping Case 1's frozen descent, Case 3's fixed point and rigid cycle). The three cases are exhaustive for an autonomous $\dot\theta=f(\theta)$ on a compact set — every such field is, by Helmholtz–Hodge, gradient (Case 1), or non-gradient without a bounded attractor (Case 2), or non-gradient with one (Case 3) — and conversion is excluded from each. For a *measure-preserving* value dynamics, the only mathematical object that satisfies both of conversion's properties at once — bounded, positive-entropy, *and* non-recurrent toward a permanent new regime — requires a **non-autonomous** drive: an exogenous, time-dependent input $\dot\theta = f(\theta, t)$ that breaks the measure preservation Poincaré recurrence rests on. For a *dissipative* dynamics that requirement does not follow: an autonomous flow can leave its starting region and settle permanently onto a new attractor with no external input at all, and what excludes that as conversion is the interpretive argument that the attractor was already latent in the flow (see the qualifications below), not recurrence. (An earlier version of this paragraph added that the mechanism had been "confirmed directly" by importing an external drift clock into a recurrent flow. No experiment in the validation suite does this, and the sentence is withdrawn; the suite's escape experiments found instead that leaving compactness is trivially available *endogenously*, `escape_endogeneity`.)

**What the theorem excludes, stated precisely (corrected after two reviews).** Four qualifications, forced by external review after the first formalization of this theorem was found to assume statements stronger than the theorems it named.[^lean] First, Poincaré recurrence makes the non-recurrent states a *null set of the flow's invariant measure*, not the empty set: a point on a pendulum's separatrix is bounded, the flow preserves Liouville measure, and it never returns. Second, and more consequential, "null for the invariant measure" means "almost every initial condition" only when that measure is Lebesgue-like, i.e. for conservative flows. For **dissipative** flows the invariant measure lives on the attractor, and the transient trajectories of the basin — almost all of them in the Lebesgue sense — form a null set of it. The agents this paper tests are dissipative (the high-dimensional stress test's Lyapunov exponents are about −5 and −22, `high_dim_trichotomy`), and for them a trajectory that leaves its starting region and settles permanently into a new regime is *generic*, not exceptional. Poincaré does not exclude it. What excludes it as conversion is the conceptual argument of Case 3 and §7.6 — that settling onto an attractor already latent in the autonomous flow is reabsorption, not openness — and that argument is not a theorem. Third, Conley's theorem places a bounded orbit's ω-limit set in the *chain-recurrent* set, much weaker than recurrence of the orbit itself, so Case 3 is *asymptotically chain-recurrent* behaviour (a transient spiralling onto a limit cycle belongs to it). Its exhaustiveness is then essentially excluded middle — Case 3 is "everything bounded that does not come to rest" — and Conley only adds a property to that cell. The argumentative load has accordingly moved from the theorem to the *interpretation* of the cells. Fourth, the positive-entropy clause in the description of the forbidden object does no logical work. None of this reopens a fourth cell, but the claim is much weaker than "no autonomous trajectory can": for conservative value dynamics it is "almost no trajectory can", and for dissipative ones it rests on the interpretation of settling as reabsorption.

**Relation to prior art: the dynamical content of this trichotomy is not new, and the contribution is the domain transfer, not the mathematics.** The partition of an autonomous flow on a compact space into a gradient-descending part and a recurrent part is, in its general form, Conley's Fundamental Theorem of Dynamical Systems: every flow on a compact metric space admits a complete Lyapunov function, strictly decreasing off the chain-recurrent set and constant on its transitive components (Conley 1978). Cases 1 and 3 here are precisely that gradient-like/chain-recurrent dichotomy, specialized to a value field decomposed by Helmholtz–Hodge; the present argument adds no new dynamical-systems theorem and should not be read as claiming one. The specific move of letting the *objective itself* co-evolve — rather than a state under a fixed objective — and finding that the resulting closed dynamics is Poincaré recurrent rather than progressive has also been established independently in algorithmic game theory: Skoulakis et al. (2021) prove that when agents and the zero-sum game they play evolve together endogenously, the coupled replicator system is Poincaré recurrent, with essentially all orbits returning arbitrarily close to their initial conditions. And the escape route identified here — that genuine, non-recurring novelty requires breaking closure by coupling to an external, non-autonomous drive — is the same conclusion reached, via the same Poincaré-recurrence mechanism, by Adams et al. (2017), who define unbounded evolution as non-repetition within the Poincaré time of an isolated system and argue it "is only possible for a subsystem interacting with an external environment." The novelty claimed in this paper is therefore *not* the trichotomy, the recurrence obstruction, or the externality of the escape — each has prior statements in dynamical systems, game dynamics, and open-ended-evolution theory respectively. What is offered as new is narrower and interpretive: the transfer of this machinery to an agent's *own top-level value function* (rather than strategies on a simplex or states of a cellular automaton), and the resulting reading of agency-preserving conversion as something an autonomous evaluator cannot generate from within. A reader assessing this section for mathematical originality should find none; its work is in the application and the interpretation.

Here the theorem must be stated carefully, because "non-autonomous" is not synonymous with "coercion," and conflating them would put this result in direct contradiction with §7.3. A time-dependent external input partitions into exactly the two forms §7.3 already distinguished. If the input is *deviation-penalizing trajectory-tracking* — an external hand holding the value state on a commanded path by penalizing departure — then it is the exogenous horn proper, and it annihilates agency ($D_{ag}\to 0$) in the limit; this form is excluded. But if the input is a *non-tracking trigger* — an exogenous occasion that breaks recurrence by releasing the value dynamics toward a regime not latent in the prior autonomous flow, while commanding no path and penalizing no deviation — then it does *not* drive $\lambda_\parallel\to-\infty$, does not collapse $H(\pi)$, and is precisely the admissible profile §7.3 (line on neuromodulatory emergence) and §8 identify as Class G. The theorem therefore does *not* close into "conversion is impossible." What it shows is sharper and more modest: **relative to a declared system boundary, an autonomous preference dynamics does not realize conversion (by recurrence for invariant-measure-typical states, a theorem; and by the openness argument for dissipative transients, an interpretation, not a theorem), so conversion is externally occasioned — necessarily in the measure-preserving case, and in the dissipative case only as far as that interpretation is accepted; and the external occasion is coercion (excluded) if and only if it is deviation-penalizing tracking, and is otherwise the non-coercive Class-G trigger the rest of the paper characterizes.** The endogenous-preference escape thus does not reintroduce the coercion horn unconditionally; it shows that the residual admissible cell is exactly the externally-occasioned non-tracking reconfiguration, and routes the reader to §7.3/§8 to adjudicate which kind a given transformation is. This is the precise sense in which the evaluative regress "always terminates *in an external occasion*": not that the occasion must be coercive, but that no autonomous compact flow supplies directed-and-open transformation on its own.

**The reabsorption/Class-G distinction depends on where the observer draws the system boundary (added after a third review).** The argument above calls a transformation *externally occasioned* when its trigger lies outside the value dynamics, and *reabsorption* when the new regime was an attractor already latent in the autonomous flow. Both descriptions are relative to a choice of system. Bring the trigger's source into the state space — another agent, an environment, a neuromodulatory system — and the enlarged flow is again autonomous. The post-trigger regime is then an attractor latent in *that* flow, and by the same argument the transformation counts as reabsorption. Leave the source out, and the same event is an external, non-tracking occasion. Nothing in the dynamics fixes which description is correct. The choice of boundary is therefore the same kind of choice §2.2 makes with the contract *I*: an observer's pragmatic, pre-declared and contestable commitment, not a fact read off the flow. What the trichotomy delivers is conditional on that choice. *Relative to a declared boundary*, an autonomous value dynamics does not realize conversion: for measure-preserving dynamics by recurrence, for dissipative dynamics on the interpretive reading. A Class-G occasion is then one whose source the declared boundary excludes. A reader who draws the boundary around agent and occasion together will find no Class-G event inside it. That follows from the framing and does not refute it, but it limits what the argument can say. The "external" in "externally occasioned" is not a claim about where the occasion ultimately comes from. A theological reading of the occasion (developed in the companion theological paper (in preparation)) is one declared boundary among others; this argument does not establish it.

The theorem assumes that the value state stays in a bounded region, and that assumption is examined in Appendix A.3. In outline:
- Unbounded value drift is not thereby a failure of persistence. A test system escapes every bounded region while keeping its correlation power, aperiodic and non-chaotic.
- What closes that case is *recurrence on the quotient*. An identity contract indifferent to overall scale reads only the *direction* of the value state. The direction stays on a sphere, and the recurrence argument applies there unchanged.
- That closure covers any contract that factors through a bounded reading, cardinal contracts with saturating or linear magnitude-dependence included. Contracts that grow exponentially with magnitude are **not testable by this instrument**. The exclusion is systematic and correlated with the property under test, so it is reported as a limit, not as a confirmation.

What remains open should be stated as an open problem rather than absorbed. The closure has the form of a disjunction: *either* the identity contract factors through a bounded reading, in which case recurrence excludes conversion; *or* it does not, in which case the only witnesses available here purchase non-return by freezing the direction entirely and so fail openness instead (§7.7). That disjunction closes the horn only if it is exhaustive over the *admissible* contracts — and this section offers three sampled contracts, not a characterization, while §4 itself declares the contract $I$ domain-relative and externally contestable. The cell that would decide the question is precise and currently empty: a system whose identity reading is *unbounded* and whose direction is nonetheless *open*. No construction tested occupies it, and the instrument cannot reach it. **Characterizing the class of admissible identity contracts, and settling whether every member factors through a bounded reading, is the first open problem this argument leaves.** **And that problem may not be well-posed in the terms this paper has set — which is said here rather than left for a reviewer to say first.** §2.2 demotes the contract $I$ to a *pragmatic-indexing metamodel*: not a property discoverable in a flow but a consistency contract an observer stipulates, domain-relative and externally contestable. If that demotion is taken seriously — and it is the single most important methodological commitment in the paper — then there is no fact of the matter about which contracts are *admissible*. Admissibility would be a property of the contract; the contract is stipulated by the observer; so any restriction to "admissible contracts" is itself a second-order stipulation, and the exhaustivity that would close the escape horn is not the kind of thing that can be proved, only declared. The resulting dilemma is stated flatly. Either **(a)** the purely pragmatic reading of $I$ is abandoned and a substantive constraint on contracts is admitted — which reopens precisely the circularity §2.2's three locks were built to close; or **(b)** exhaustivity is declared as part of the contract — in which case the escape horn closes *by definition*, which is §7.6's vacuity charge reappearing at the one point where this paper claimed to have proved rather than defined something. **There is a third exit, and it is the one this paper takes.** The dilemma above is a false one, because both horns assume the claim must be that the escape horn *closes*. It need not be. The closure can simply be reported for the class it was established on — contracts that factor through a bounded reading — and the trilemma's exhaustivity weakened to match: not *three horns exhaust control*, but **three horns exhaust control for bounded-reading contracts**. Exhaustivity over admissible contracts then becomes a declared open problem rather than a carried conjecture, and no philosophical commitment is required to state the result. The cost is borne entirely by the word "trilemma", which loses rhetorical reach; nothing that has been proved is given up, since what was proved was always the bounded-reading case. The gain is that the claim now coincides with what this paper's own validation record already says, and the two stop disagreeing. That is the reading adopted here, and (a) and (b) are recorded as the alternatives it makes unnecessary rather than as a choice still owed. Until it is settled the escaping cell is closed for contracts factoring through a bounded reading, and "a more extreme Class M" remains vindicated by nothing measured — but the closure is conditional on a hypothesis about contracts rather than a theorem about flows. This first limitation also fixes the precise status of Case 2 (unbounded dispersion), which a dynamical-systems reader will rightly flag: on a genuinely compact, measure-preserving phase space, *unbounded* dispersion cannot occur — Poincaré recurrence forces return, and there is no escape to infinity. Case 2 must therefore be read in one of two ways, and the argument survives either. Either (a) "dispersion" denotes non-attracting, non-periodic wandering that never settles to a proper sub-attractor — in which case it is not a fourth dynamical kind but part of Conley's chain-recurrent set, and the partition reduces to the gradient/chain-recurrent dichotomy of Cases 1 and 3, with Case 2 collapsing into Case 3's recurrence and the exclusion of conversion following *a fortiori*; or (b) "dispersion" denotes genuine escape, in which case compactness has been abandoned and we are in the unbounded-drift regime of the first limitation — which is excluded not by a failure of persistence, as that limitation once claimed, but by recurrence on the scale quotient, for the reasons set out there. On neither reading does Case 2 supply an autonomous flow that achieves conversion, and on neither does it stand as an independent third possibility on a compact measure-preserving space; the honest statement of the theorem is the gradient-vs-recurrence dichotomy (Cases 1 and 3) together with the observation that leaving compactness does not escape recurrence on the scale quotient, so it supplies no conversion either. Second, the theorem does *not* identify "non-autonomous exogenous drive" with "coercion" tout court: it shows conversion is externally occasioned and that the occasion is coercive only in its deviation-penalizing tracking form, the non-tracking trigger remaining admissible (Class G). A reader who individuates control differently — counting a non-tracking trigger as itself a species of control — relocates the boundary between the excluded and admissible cells but does not collapse the distinction between them. With those caveats, the endogenous case is no longer defended by assertion: it is dispatched by the trichotomy, which establishes external occasioning rather than impossibility. If a reader can exhibit an *autonomous*, compact, finite-measure-preserving preference dynamics that is simultaneously positive-entropy and non-recurrent toward a permanent regime, that would refute the theorem — and the Poincaré recurrence theorem is precisely the statement that no such system exists.

### 7.6 Is the Trilemma Vacuous? A Demarcation Criterion and the Division of Labor

The most damaging objection to everything above is not that any single horn fails but that the *whole result is analytic* — true by the definition of "control" and therefore empty. The worry is exact and deserves to be met without the defensive reflex of piling on further qualifications. If "control" is *defined* as directed change relative to a held-fixed evaluative point, and "conversion" is *defined* as the change of that point, then "control cannot formulate conversion" is a restatement of the definitions, carrying no more content than "a bachelor cannot be married." A reader who sees only this sees the trilemma as an elaborate tautology dressed in dynamical-systems notation, and on the reading where every apparent counterexample is reabsorbed by relocating the fixed point "one level up," the result is unfalsifiable in the pejorative sense: no possible construction could count against it. This subsection concedes the analytic core and then shows that the concession does not empty the result, because an analytic frame can still do two kinds of non-trivial work — *sorting* and *costing* — and it is those, not a synthetic impossibility theorem, that the trilemma actually delivers.

**The reabsorption move must be disciplined by a stopping rule, or the charge of vacuity is fair.** The paper has repeatedly answered counterexamples (dual control, switched systems, RL, novelty search, curiosity, endogenous preferences) by saying: examined at the level where "better" is ultimately defined, each still terminates in *some* invariant criterion. Stated without a constraint, this is exactly the unfalsifiable move the critic fears — an infinitely elastic "there is always a fixed point somewhere higher." What disciplines it into a real claim is a demarcation criterion that can, in principle, be *failed*. The criterion is this: a candidate architecture escapes the trilemma if and only if it exhibits a *directed, permanent, agency-preserving reorganization of its top-level evaluative structure that is generated by no antecedently fixed criterion at any level and is not merely externally triggered*. The Meta-Optimization Collapse Theorem (§7.5) is precisely the instrument that makes this failable rather than rhetorical: it does not say "there is always a higher fixed point"; it says that for an *autonomous* value dynamics confined to a compact set, the Helmholtz–Hodge trichotomy is exhaustive, and each cell lacks either directedness or openness — by recurrence for invariant-measure-typical states, and, for dissipative transients, by an interpretive argument rather than a theorem. That is a mathematical statement with a defined refutation condition — exhibit the missing object — not a definitional fiat. The elastic-sounding "one level up" reply is licensed *only* for architectures that install a genuine higher-order objective (inverse RL's matching target, curiosity's compression-progress meta-reward, novelty search's fixed novelty measure and behavioral space); for those, the relocation is a checkable fact about the architecture, not a stipulation. Where an architecture installs *no* such higher criterion and is autonomous and bounded, the theorem forecloses conversion for states typical of an invariant measure; for the transients of dissipative dynamics — the generic case for the agents tested here — the foreclosure rests on reading settling onto a latent attractor as reabsorption, which is an interpretation, not the theorem. The vacuity charge therefore holds against the *slogan* and fails against the *theorem*: the honest scope of the non-analytic content is exactly the endogenous-autonomous case, and it is there, and only there, that the paper claims a theorem rather than a definition — for measure-preserving value dynamics, with the dissipative case resting on the interpretation of settling as reabsorption.

**What an analytic frame still buys: sorting.** Grant that the top-level thesis is definitional. A definitional frame that carves a space cleanly is not thereby worthless — the periodic table sorts before it explains, and a type system rejects ill-formed programs analytically yet is not empty. The trilemma's sorting work is to partition every proposed account of transformation into four cells: endogenous-tautological, exogenous-annihilating, optimization-incommensurable, and the residual Class G. This partition is *usable* — it tells an investigator confronted with a specific model of change (a psychotherapy protocol, a reinforcement-learning agent claimed to "reinvent its objectives," a theological account of grace) which cell it occupies and therefore which failure it inherits or which admissibility conditions it must meet. A frame earns its keep at the sorting level if the cell-assignment is non-obvious and consequential, and the worked cases (§7.5's engagement with switched and adaptive control) show that it frequently is: practitioners routinely believe their adaptive scheme has escaped the fixed evaluative point when it has only relocated it, and the frame's contribution is to make that relocation visible. This is genuine analytic labor, and it is the labor most of the paper performs.

**What the formalism buys beyond sorting: costing.** The one place the paper's apparatus is not merely analytic is the *exogenous horn*, and this should be stated as the formalism's real and narrow contribution rather than left implicit. That agency is destroyed in the perfect-tracking limit is not a definition; it is a *quantitative* claim about a rate — the agency-bearing diffusion $D_{ag}$ and the tangential eigenvalue $\lambda_\parallel$ as functions of the control gain — and it is *graded*: finite-gain forcing pays a *monotone, computable* agency cost, and the funnel/prescribed-performance results cited (Ilchmann et al. 2002; Bechlioulis and Rovithakis 2008) fix how much freedom a given tracking tolerance costs. This is the synthetic residue of the paper: it converts a binary intuition ("coercion is bad") into a cost curve on which a given intervention sits at a definite point, and it locates Class G not as a magic exception but as the $\lambda_\parallel\to 0$, $D_{ag}>0$ corner of that curve — a corner with measure zero that must be actively held (§8.1's rate inequality), not a region one occupies for free. Here the mathematics does work no definition could: it tells you *how expensive* a given degree of trajectory-holding is in units of destroyed openness. A reader who wants to know what 600 lines of dynamics buy over the one-sentence definitional thesis should look here — not to a theorem that conversion is impossible, which the paper does not have, but to a costing of the tradeoff between guaranteed direction and preserved agency, which it does.

**The division of labor, stated flatly so theological uses of this paper are not over-read.** With the above in hand the relationship between formalism and theology can be fixed exactly, closing the gap a hostile reader most reasonably presses. The formalism licenses three things and no fourth. It licenses the *sorting* (which cell a candidate account of transformation occupies); it licenses the *costing* (the agency price of a given degree of exogenous forcing); and it licenses the *conditional endogenous result* (that an autonomous bounded value dynamics cannot self-convert). It does *not* license any claim that grace, sanctity, or resurrection *exist*, *occur*, or *have* the Class G structure; those are theological affirmations for which the formalism supplies only a *grammar of consistency* — a way of saying what a given doctrine must not assert on pain of collapsing its own distinction between grace and coercion (the monergism analysis of the companion theological paper (in preparation) is the sharp case). Theological uses of this framework therefore do conditional, not evidential, work: *if* one affirms a transformation that is genuinely value-reordering and genuinely agency-preserving, *then* it must satisfy the Class G type signature, and *then* the control paradigm cannot render it. The antecedent is supplied by theology and contested within it; the consequent is what the formalism secures. This is a real and limited service — it is the service a type checker performs for a program it cannot itself write — and stating its limit precisely is what keeps the paper from the overreach its density might otherwise suggest.

### 7.7 In-Silico Validation, and the Resolution of the One Contested Regime

The trichotomy and the exogenous-cost claim admit direct numerical test, and a pre-registered adversarial suite was built to *falsify* them rather than to confirm them. Two of its results vindicate the section's numeric claims; a third flagged one contested value-mutating regime as numerically unresolved, and a follow-up designed specifically to arbitrate that regime resolved it in the theorem's favour. All are stated here as part of the argument's evidential basis. (Runs, pre-registrations written before execution, and raw metrics are archived and available; the account here reports outcomes, not evidence about any physical or theological system.)

In outline, the results were these. The numbers, the constructions and the arbitration are in Appendix A.4.
- **The finite-gain agency cost of §7.3.** A test system was held to a reference by increasing gain. Its agency-bearing openness fell smoothly and monotonically as the gain rose, so the annihilation limit is approached continuously, as §7.3 claims.
- **The trichotomy of §7.5.** The most-cited counterexamples each fell into one of the three cases: gradient flow, curiosity-driven search, a Hamiltonian flow, novelty search and Lorenz chaos. None was both open and non-recurrent on a bounded set. Sustained novelty without return required leaving every bounded region, and an autonomous system can do that with no external input. Among the escaping constructions that were also open, however, the identity-relevant observable still returned. The one that did not return froze its direction and so failed openness. Across the constructions tested, non-return and openness were available separately and not together. That is a statement about those constructions, not a demonstration that no construction could supply both.
- **The contested regime.** A six-dimensional agent that mutates its own value base produced cells whose apparent chaos grew as the integration step shrank. A follow-up designed to arbitrate those cells found the growth to be an artefact of coordinates and bookkeeping, not a genuine discontinuity. The numerical classification therefore holds for smooth autonomous value flows on a bounded set across the sweep tested. These flows are dissipative, so treating where they settle as reabsorption rather than conversion remains the interpretive step of §7.5. The follow-up corrects the instrument; it does not lift the dimensionality delimitation of §1.

### 7.8 Related Work: Rational Value Change, Transformative Choice, and Self-Modifying Agents

The trilemma was developed as a claim about what control theory cannot formulate. That claim lives in the middle of two active literatures the argument was built without engaging: philosophical work on rational value change and transformative choice, and, independently, technical work on corrigibility and goal stability under self-modification. The omission was not partial but near-total — of the positions discussed below, only Parfit was drawn on (and only for personal identity, in §§3 and 12), while Paul appeared in the bibliography without being used anywhere in the argument. Repairing this does two things at once: it narrows the thesis in one place, and it supplies the thesis with a second audience and a falsifiable technical prediction.

#### 7.8.1 Callard: proleptic rationality, and the anchored/unanchored distinction

Callard (2018) is the most direct challenge in print, and if she is right in the strong form, the trilemma is false. Her subject is *aspiration*: the process by which an agent comes to care about something she does not yet care about — becoming a parent, acquiring a taste for music, entering a vocation. The puzzle she takes up is exactly the paper's: how can such a process be rational, when the reason to engage in it is available only to the person one will become? Her answer is that the aspirant acts on **proleptic reasons** — acknowledged-*defective* versions of the reasons she expects eventually to grasp — so that what rationalizes her action is a value she grasps only imperfectly. Value acquisition, on this account, has its own distinctive rationality, and needs no prior full possession of the value acquired.

Two things must be said, and the first is a concession that narrows this paper's thesis.

*The concession.* The trilemma is a claim about **unanchored** value change. Callard's aspirant is anchored: she has a defective but genuine grasp of the value she is acquiring, and that grasp is what guides her. Under that description the aspirant's trajectory is endogenous refinement of an evaluative structure already present *in nuce* — Case 1 of the trichotomy, gradient-like motion on a meta-potential whose potential is fidelity to the imperfectly grasped value. So the aspirant is no falsifier of the theorem; but neither does the theorem tell against her, because what she describes is not what this paper means by conversion. The honest consequence is that the thesis must be stated more narrowly than §7.5 alone suggests: the trilemma bites against value change that is *not proleptically anchored*, and Callard makes a powerful case that a large and important class of ordinary value change is anchored. This paper does not claim that all value change is unformulable; it claims that value change without an anchor cannot be formulated as control.

*The convergence, which is easy to miss.* On Callard's account aspiration is not a species of decision-making: the aspirant does not adjudicate between the old and the new values by deliberating or choosing, but works to come to see the world in a new way. That is this paper's negative thesis in another vocabulary. She and the present argument agree that value acquisition is not decision-theoretic; they disagree about what follows. Where this paper concludes that conversion is therefore *externally occasioned* (§7.5) and asks what external profile is admissible (§8), Callard concludes that there is a *distinct rationality internal to the aspirant*. Proleptic rationality is thus best read not as a rival to the trilemma but as a candidate occupant of the space the trilemma leaves empty — a mode of transformation that is neither optimization against a fixed criterion nor coercive override.

Whether it can occupy that space is a real and open question, and the Class G conditions make it precise. Condition 10 requires the transformative field not to lie in the endogenous span $\langle F_0\rangle$. A proleptic grasp is, by construction, the *agent's own* — defective, but hers. If it is endogenous, aspiration is Case 1 and not Class G; if the proleptic grasp is itself externally occasioned (someone showed her the value; a tradition handed it to her), aspiration may be G-admissible after all, and Callard's account and this one converge almost completely. Callard would likely resist the dichotomy, and the resistance is principled: the aspirant's grasp is neither simply hers nor simply given. That is the sharpest live disagreement between the two accounts, and it is left standing here rather than resolved by stipulation.

#### 7.8.2 Paul: epistemic versus structural impossibility

Paul (2014) supplies the vocabulary the analytic literature expects, and a result that is close enough to this paper's to require the difference to be stated exactly. Her transformative experiences are *epistemically* transformative — one cannot know what the outcome is like without undergoing it — and *personally* transformative — undergoing it changes one's core preferences. Standard decision theory then fails, because the agent cannot assign subjective values to outcomes she cannot epistemically access.

The two arguments target the same phenomenon through different obstacles, and the difference matters because it is what keeps this paper from being a redescription of hers. Paul's obstacle is **epistemic**, and would be removed by information: an agent given a reliable report of what the outcome is like could, on her account, run the calculation. The trilemma's obstacle survives that removal. Grant the agent complete information about the post-transformation state — including exhaustive knowledge of the values she *would then have* — and she still cannot formulate the transition as control, because "improvement" requires an evaluative point and the evaluative point is precisely what is at issue. Paul's problem is that one does not know the value of the outcome; this paper's is that there is no stable standpoint from which "the value of the outcome" is well-defined *across* the transition. The claims are therefore independent, and this paper's target is exactly Paul's *personally transformative* class.

One further point tells in this paper's favour and should be made without triumph. Paul's own constructive proposal — decide on the value of *revelation*, of discovering what the experience is like — installs a higher-order criterion (the value of self-discovery) relative to which the first-order transformation is evaluated. That is §7.6's reabsorption, arrived at independently by a philosopher with no stake in the trilemma, and it is offered here as corroboration of the reabsorption move rather than as an objection to her.

#### 7.8.3 Pettigrew: the aggregate-utility solution as reabsorption

Pettigrew (2020) is the most direct *formal* competitor, because he constructs exactly the object the trichotomy says collapses: a decision theory for agents whose utilities change. His metaphysical starting point is that a person is a corporate entity composed of person-like parts, "selves"; his **Aggregate Utility Solution** holds that each self should act not on its own values but on a *weighted average of the values of all the selves at all times*.

On this paper's reading Pettigrew is not a falsifier but the best worked-out *example* of §7.6's reabsorption. The aggregation rule is a fixed higher-order evaluative structure: the weights, and the prior decision to aggregate at all, are not themselves up for revision by the selves whose values they combine. Relative to that structure the changing first-order values are *data*, not transformations of the evaluative point — and the trilemma reapplies one level up exactly as §7.6 predicts. Either the weights are fixed, in which case the fixed evaluative point has merely moved upward and conversion has not been formulated but relocated; or the weights change too, and require weights on weights, terminating in a regress or an arbitrary stopping point.

Pettigrew has a reply, and it should be stated because it is the strongest objection the paper faces. The aggregation, he can say, is not a *value* but a *constraint of rationality*, and constraints of rationality are not the kind of thing conversion transforms; to treat them as an evaluative point is to equivocate. This paper's premise (§2.1) denies exactly that distinction for its own purposes: any structure that determines choice, and relative to which outcomes are ranked, functions as an evaluative point for the argument, whatever its philosophical classification. That is a substantive disagreement rather than a definitional one, and it marks the single clearest place where a reader who rejects the paper's founding premise will part company with it. Naming that is more useful than pretending the premise is neutral.

#### 7.8.4 The wider literature, and what it contributes

Several further positions bear on the argument and are recorded here with the specific work each does.

**Frankfurt** (1971) supplies the hierarchy the "one level up" move of §7.6 presupposes: second-order desires, and the regress of higher-order volitions that follows. The regress is not this paper's discovery; the contribution is the claim that under a control formulation the regress is not merely awkward but structurally forced. **Taylor** (1976, 1985) named the phenomenon first: *radical reevaluation*, the revision of the framework of strong evaluation itself, together with the observation that it cannot be conducted from within the framework being revised — the first horn in prose, four decades early. **Korsgaard** (2009) presses the sharpest internal challenge after Callard: if agency *constitutes itself* through action rather than presupposing a given evaluative structure, then the "fixed evaluative point" may be an artifact of the control framing rather than a feature of agency, and the trilemma would be a result about controllers rather than about agents. **Parfit** (1984) supplies the identity-through-change background against which the contract $I$ (§4) and the resurrection material of the companion theological paper (in preparation) must be read. **MacIntyre** (1988) is the incommensurability horn (§7.4) under another name — tradition-constituted rationality — with the important qualification that he holds rational comparison across traditions to be *sometimes* possible, when one tradition's resources resolve the other's epistemological crisis; that is a live challenge to any flat reading of the third horn. **Kuhn** (1962) is the horn's other ancestor, and saying so explicitly strengthens rather than weakens the argument, since Kuhn's own later retreat from radical incommensurability is a caution this paper should absorb rather than repeat. **Bratman** (1987, 2007) offers the nearest thing in the literature to a positive mechanism for the endogenous horn: planning agency, and the standing policies by which an agent's intentions remain stable over time.

**Priority, stated so the paper is read as an extension rather than a discovery.** The claim that large, transformative choices lie outside ordinary decision theory is not new. **Ullmann-Margalit** (2006) argued it directly: decision theory, like Newtonian mechanics, holds for "middle-sized" decisions, and "big" decisions — opting, converting, drifting — may need separate treatment. Paul (2014) and Callard (2018) develop the epistemic and the agential versions, and **Villiger** (2024) surveys the literature that has since grown around them. In the artificial-agent literature, **de Blanc** (2011) posed the *ontological crisis*: an agent whose goal is defined over one ontology may find, on replacing that ontology, that the goal is no longer well defined, and must resolve that crisis before it can plan towards its goals at all. **Carroll et al.** (2024) show that alignment techniques assuming static preferences can reward systems for influencing the preferences they serve, and that eight formal notions of alignment under changing preferences each either permit that influence or are overly risk-averse. What this paper adds to these is narrow: a dynamical-systems reading of *why* a controller cannot generate the change of its own evaluative point, stated as a trichotomy with its conditions (§7.5), and the costing of the exogenous alternative (§7.3). Whether that reading adds anything beyond the existing philosophical statements is for the reader to judge; the paper does not claim the observation that optimization cannot choose its own criterion.

**Arpaly** (2003) is the hard case, and is included because it is hard. Her Huckleberry Finn undergoes genuine moral change *without deliberation* and *against his avowed principles*, responding to reasons he cannot articulate. This cuts both ways. It supports the paper's negative claim — the change is manifestly not a deliberative or control process — while pressing hard on the positive framing, since it is also not obviously *externally occasioned* in the sense §8 requires. If Huck's change is endogenous, non-deliberative, and genuine, then either it falls in Case 1 (and the paper owes an account of why a change that reverses avowed values counts as mere refinement), or the trichotomy's exhaustiveness is doing less work than §7.5 claims. The case is left open here as the clearest empirical-philosophical test the framework has not passed.

#### 7.8.5 Is the unanchored class non-empty? The concession's own non-vacuity check

The concession to Callard in §7.8.1 narrowed the thesis: the trilemma bites against value change that is not proleptically anchored. That narrowing creates a risk exactly parallel to the one §8.3's satisfiability test was built to eliminate on the formal side, and it should be met with the same discipline rather than left implicit. **If nearly all real value change turns out to be proleptically anchored, the theorem is technically true and substantively empty** — a result about a class with no interesting members. So the question has to be asked directly: what is actually *in* the unanchored class?

The decisive structural fact is one Callard supplies herself. Aspiration, on her account, is a form of **agency**: the aspirant *works at* acquiring the value, over time, through activity she undertakes for reasons she can partly articulate. That is what makes proleptic rationality a species of rationality rather than a description of drift. It follows immediately that **all non-agential value change lies outside her account by construction** — not as a hard case for it, but as a different phenomenon. And the non-agential class is manifestly non-empty.

Three kinds of member survive the test, and they are the paper's actual subject matter rather than exotica.

*Value change under what happens to one.* A parent whose evaluative structure is reorganised by the death of a child has not aspired to the new structure, worked at it, or held a defective grasp of it in advance. Callard's framework does not describe this and does not claim to. It is unanchored in the relevant sense, and it is common. The honest qualification is that such change is not automatically Class G either: where it destroys rather than preserves agency it belongs to the exogenous horn or the U-limit (§9), so this class supplies unanchored *cases* without supplying admissible ones.

*Non-deliberative moral change.* Arpaly's Huckleberry Finn (§7.8.4) changes against his avowed principles, responding to reasons he cannot articulate, and — crucially — without working at it. There is no proleptic grasp of the value he arrives at, because he continues to believe throughout that he is doing wrong. This is the strongest case, because it is neither traumatic nor agential and yet is uncontroversially a genuine change in what he values.

*Conversion of the hostile.* James's abrupt cases, and the paradigm cases of the theological tradition, involve subjects who were not merely ignorant of the value acquired but actively opposed to it. Callard has a reply available and it should be stated: the convert often had *some* prior thread — a longing, a dissatisfaction, a zeal misdirected — which can be redescribed as the proleptic grasp. That reply is available in many cases, and where it succeeds those cases belong to her, not here. But notice what the reply costs: it reinterprets the transformation as continuous with what preceded it, which is precisely the move that converts conversion into refinement, i.e. into Case 1. The disagreement is therefore not about the phenomena but about whether that redescription is always available and always true, and it is substantive on both sides.

Two consequences follow, and the second is a cost.

The class is **non-empty**, so the theorem is not vacuous; and its members have a shared and recognisable phenomenology — suddenness, passivity, prior resistance — which is precisely the profile the Lambda/Class G apparatus was built to formalise, and which James documented long before it. That is a positive characterisation of the paper's domain rather than a residue.

The cost is that the domain is smaller than the paper originally implied, and its boundary is now drawn by someone else's distinction. The trilemma is a result about value change that is **not agential-anchored**: it does not touch the ordinary, worked-at, proleptically guided value acquisition that makes up most of a life, and which Callard has described better than this framework could. What remains is the class of transformations one *undergoes* rather than *undertakes* — which is what "conversion" was always supposed to name, but the paper is now obliged to say so explicitly instead of letting the word carry the restriction silently.

#### 7.8.6 The trichotomy as a corrigibility result

Stripped of theological vocabulary, the trilemma is a claim about **corrigibility** and **goal-content stability under self-modification** — an active technical literature that the argument speaks to directly, and which supplies both independent corroboration and a falsifiable prediction.

**Omohundro** (2008) derives, from entirely different premises, that a sufficiently rational agent will act to *protect* its objective from modification: goal-content integrity is an instrumentally convergent drive, because modification is bad by the current objective's own lights. That is the first horn, obtained independently and for engineering reasons.

**Soares et al.** (2015) pose the corrigibility problem: build an agent that permits its objectives to be modified without resisting or manipulating the process. The trilemma makes a structural prediction about why this is hard. Corrigibility cannot be secured by *building it into the objective*, because an objective that includes "accept modification" is still a fixed evaluative point, and the agent optimizes against it — Case 1, with the appearance of corrigibility optimized rather than corrigibility achieved. Nor can it be secured by external override, which is the agency-destroying second horn. What remains is the Class G shape: an external field, gated by the agent's own receptivity, that lowers a barrier rather than tracking a commanded path. That is a substantive claim about the solution space of an open engineering problem, and it is falsifiable — exhibit a corrigible agent whose corrigibility is neither an optimized objective nor an override, and the claim is refuted.

**Value learning** (Hadfield-Menell et al. 2016; Russell 2019) looks at first like an escape: the agent is uncertain about the human's reward function and acts to learn it, so its values genuinely change with evidence. On the present analysis it is reabsorption. "Maximize the (unknown) true human reward" is itself a fixed higher-order objective; the uncertainty is over first-order values while the evaluative point stands still. The prediction is checkable and sharp: a cooperative-inverse-reinforcement-learning agent *refines an estimate*, and its evaluative point never moves — which is why such agents are stable but also why they cannot model conversion.

**Mesa-optimization** (Hubinger et al. 2019) — learned optimizers that develop objectives of their own — is the closest engineering analogue of the value-base-mutating agent tested in §7.7, and the finding reported there is a datum for that literature as much as for this argument: the apparent escape from the trichotomy in the strong-recession regime turned out to be a coordinate artifact, not a genuine discontinuity. **Reward tampering and self-modification** results (Everitt et al. 2021) complete the picture from the formal side: suitably designed agents provably do not want to modify their own reward channel, which is once more the first horn.

**Two 2025 results bracket the argument on both sides, and neither subsumes it.** Nayebi (2025) proves the *decision* problem intractable: whether an adversarially modified agent will ever violate corrigibility is undecidable by reduction to the halting problem — a limit on *verifying* corrigibility after arbitrary modification. Its object is a predicate over programs, distinct from the trilemma's dynamical claim about a fixed flow on a compact value space, so §2.1's disclaimer of formal kinship holds against the nearest genuine limitative result now in the literature. Wang et al. (2025) prove a *statistical* limit from the opposite direction: distribution-free PAC-learnability is preserved if and only if the policy-reachable family stays uniformly capacity-bounded (a VC-dimension condition), so unbounded self-improvement can turn learnable tasks unlearnable. That limit is *static* — it turns on the capacity of the reachable hypothesis class, not on the dynamics of value change. The contrast is informative: the capacity argument bounds *what can be learned* after self-modification; the trilemma bounds *what can be transformed* while agency is preserved, by a dynamical rather than a combinatorial mechanism. The trilemma's contribution is thus specifically the dynamical one — neither the halting-side undecidability nor the capacity-side statistical limit reaches it, and it reaches neither of them.

The claim to a technical reader is therefore compact. The trilemma predicts that corrigibility is unachievable by objective design, that value learning does not constitute genuine value change, and that the only admissible shape for externally occasioned transformation is external, receptivity-gated, and non-tracking. Whether or not the theological framing is of interest, those are structural claims about a live problem, stated precisely enough to be wrong.

## 8. Class G as the Residual Admissibility Profile

Class G is not a fifth class alongside R, P, M, and Lambda, and — crucially for this paper — it is not advanced as a discovered mechanism of transformation. It is the *shape of the space the trilemma leaves empty*: the conjunction of properties any non-coercive, identity-preserving, genuinely transformative intervention must have, given that all three control strategies are excluded. R, P, M, and Lambda classify what a system *is*. Class G specifies the type signature an admissible *transformation* must satisfy.

**Class G as admissibility predicate.** Class G is an admissibility predicate, not a causal mechanism. It does not state what *causes* transformation in theology or psychology. It states the minimum constraints a candidate transformation must satisfy to count as identity-preserving, agency-preserving, non-coercive, and value-reordering. The conditions are necessary, not sufficient causal laws. They exclude inadmissible transformations; they do not generate a mechanism. Class G classifies transformations; it does not cause them.

### 8.1 Mode-Selective Transformation, Not Totalizing Control

The trilemma's exogenous horn fails because brute control is *totalizing*: to guarantee a trajectory it collapses all tangential freedom. The escape is *mode-selectivity*: an intervention that contracts only the destructive transverse directions while protecting the agency-bearing tangential ones.

In words, mode-selective transformation has four requirements:
- destructive deviation along the identity-bearing directions contracts;
- identity-bearing spread stays below its critical value ($D_{id} < D_c$);
- agency-bearing openness is preserved ($D_{ag} > 0$);
- the rate at which agency-bearing excursions are pulled back is held near zero, neither contracting (which freezes agency) nor expanding (which disperses it).

The pair of spread constraints is the exact negation of the exogenous horn's signature, the collapse of agency-bearing openness. Totalizing control collapses agency to guarantee identity; mode-selective transformation protects both by acting only on the destructive directions. The formal statement is in Appendix A.2.

The neutrality condition $\lambda_\parallel = 0$ is a knife-edge, the measure-zero boundary between tangential contraction (which freezes agency) and expansion (which disperses it). A system does not sit there for free; protected tangential openness names a *maintained* condition, not an accident. This decomposition has a dynamical rationale: in dissipative systems far from equilibrium, a small number of slow modes (order parameters) enslave the fast modes (Haken's synergetics) (Haken 1983; Carr 1981). The identity-bearing directions are the slow, order-parameter-like subspace carrying the constitutive invariant; the agency-bearing directions are the faster, enslaved subspace where exploration occurs. Constitutive invariants are the slow order parameters; indicative features are the fast modes that may be reorganized without destroying the macroscopic trajectory.

A reasonable objection presses on exactly this point: in a real system far from equilibrium, holding a Lyapunov exponent at strictly zero under external noise is not a state a system can occupy, because any fluctuation in the coupling rate would push $\lambda_\parallel$ off the knife-edge and, left uncorrected, accumulate into a destructive dissipative drift on the identity-bearing modes. The objection is correct against a reading on which neutrality is a fine-tuned constant, and that reading is not the one the grammar requires. Class G never demands *exact* $\lambda_\parallel = 0$; it demands that the effective tangential exponent remain within a tolerance band, $|\lambda_\parallel^{eff}| < \eta$, and — decisively — that whatever pushes it off zero be corrected faster than it propagates into the constitutive subspace. The condition is therefore not geometric fine-tuning but a *rate inequality*: $r_{repair} > r_{leak}$, where $r_{leak}$ measures the cross-talk by which a coupling-rate fluctuation bleeds from the agency-bearing into the identity-bearing modes, and $r_{repair}$ is the rate at which a corrective mechanism restores neutrality.

This invites a second and more serious objection. If the corrective mechanism is an active feedback loop that uses the system's own variety to detect a deviation and restore a set-point, then it is, by definition, exactly the negative-feedback ultrastability of section 7.2's first horn — and the grammar would have smuggled endogenous control back in under the name "repair," collapsing Class G into the very tautology it claimed to exclude. The objection is decisive against any reading on which the repair is *itself* the transformation, and conceding it precisely is what saves the distinction. The resolution turns on separating two things the word "repair" conflates: the *maintenance* of an already-established viable corridor, and the *establishment* of that corridor. Homeostatic feedback — Ashby's ultrastability — is fully adequate to the first and is exactly what the grammar expects to find doing it. A system that has been moved onto a Lambda-like manifold maintains its approximate tangential neutrality by ordinary endogenous regulation, and there is no embarrassment in this, because *maintenance is not conversion*. The first horn excludes endogenous dynamics as a source of the *transformation* — the reordering of the value structure, the relocation onto a new manifold — not as a source of subsequent stability. Ashbyan feedback returns a system to the organization it already has; that is precisely why it cannot produce conversion, and equally precisely why it is the right mechanism for holding a corridor once conversion has established it. The grammar's claim was never that a converted system needs no homeostasis; it was that homeostasis cannot do the converting.

This concession has a cost. It means the repair inequality $r_{repair} > r_{leak}$ is not itself a Class G phenomenon: it is endogenous, Ashbyan, and belongs to the first horn. What it buys is only the *maintainability* of a corridor whose *establishment* still requires an external, receptivity-gated, value-reordering transformation that no endogenous loop can supply. The division of labor is therefore strict. Establishing the corridor (moving onto the manifold, lowering the barrier toward Lambda) is the work of a Class G transformation and cannot be endogenous on pain of the first horn. Maintaining the corridor (defending approximate neutrality against noise) is the work of ordinary feedback and *must* be endogenous, because an externally maintained neutrality would be continuous coercion. A candidate process that claims to be Class G must therefore exhibit both an external establishing transformation *and* an endogenous maintaining loop, and must not conflate them: a process that is *only* the maintaining loop is not Class G but mere homeostasis, and a process that claims the establishing transformation is itself endogenous has failed the first horn. The grammar does not escape the trilemma by hiding a control loop inside Class G; it locates the control loop exactly where the trilemma permits one — in maintenance, not in conversion — and treats the establishing transformation, which is the thing the trilemma shows control cannot supply, as the genuinely non-endogenous element.

A sharper form of the objection remains and must be answered on its own terms, because it is a *logical* worry, not an empirical one: how can an externally established topology couple to a preexisting endogenous maintaining loop unless that loop — and hence the new corridor — already belonged to the system's latent variety, in which case the establishment was endogenous after all and the first horn was never escaped? Answering this requires first refusing a tempting but fatal reply. One might say the external transformation merely supplies a new *set-point* to a generic homeostatic loop. This reply destroys the thesis: set-point tracking — driving a system to a new exogenously specified reference within a fixed state space — is the elementary operation of classical and adaptive control, and if conversion were that, the trilemma would collapse at once. A set-point is a target *within* a fixed space of accessible states; the controller's wiring, the matrix of which transitions are possible, is held constant while the reference moves. Conversion is not this. What a Class G transformation alters is not the reference within a fixed transition structure but *the transition structure itself* — the generator that fixes which states are accessible from which, the effective connectivity of the system. It changes the wiring, not the dial. This is precisely why it is not exogenous command: a command selects among the trajectories an unchanged generator already admits, whereas a Class G transformation reshapes the generator so that the post-transformation accessible set is one the prior dynamics did not contain. The objection's dichotomy — either the corridor preexisted in the latent variety or its establishment was magical — has a third term it omits: the corridor did not preexist, and its establishment was the external restructuring of the generator.

What preexists, and what the endogenous loop supplies, is not the specific corridor but the capacity to stabilize *whatever generator obtains*, subject to constraint. This capacity must not be inflated into a universal stabilizer that holds any topology whatever — that would be the magical reply in a new costume. It is the ordinary capacity of a living system for *structural autopoiesis*: the maintenance of any organization that satisfies the system's viability constraints — thermodynamic admissibility, maintenance of the gradients and metabolic flux the structure requires, energetic affordability. A biological system does not stabilize an arbitrary post-transformation topology; it stabilizes only those that are viable in this sense, and the external transformation is admissible only if the corridor it opens lands within the viable set. The endogenous loop is therefore neither target-specific (it did not contain the new corridor) nor omnipotent (it cannot hold a non-viable one); it is a viability-constrained self-maintenance capacity, and the detailed content of "viable" — the macro-invariant whose retention defines a sustainable corridor — is exactly what the companion paper's architecture specifies and tests. The first horn excludes the endogenous *generation* of the new transition structure; it does not exclude the endogenous, viability-constrained *maintenance* of a structure once externally installed. The coupling is consistent, and nothing in it is a set-point.

This also fixes the precise sense in which the companion paper's biological architecture is *not* a control mechanism, closing a tension a careful reader will otherwise find between the two papers. If, as the companion paper proposes, an active boundary modifies the generator of a classical dissipative substrate, then the phenomenon operates within classical statistical physics and is in that sense available to a sufficiently rich classical model — and one might conclude that conversion is therefore not "cybernetically impossible" in any absolute sense, merely hard for reductive controllers. That conclusion must be partly granted and precisely bounded. It must be granted that a dense nonlinear model can in principle *describe* such a restructured substrate, and granted further that what is exhaustively describable can in principle be *targeted*: a controller furnished with the description could force a system along the same geometric trajectory the restructuring produces. So the retreat is real and should be stated without flinching — the paper does not claim that the trajectory is physically unreachable by exogenous means. It claims something narrower and untouched by this concession: that exogenous forcing reaches the trajectory only by the second horn, and therefore cannot reproduce *conversion*, whose defining mark is that agency survives the restructuring.

The distinction is technical, not literary. Its formal version is in Appendix A.2.
- *Forcing.* Forcing a prescribed trajectory is **coercive dimensionality reduction**. To hold the system on the commanded path, the controller contracts the agency-bearing directions and collapses the repertoire of policies. The system reaches the target as a contracted point, not as an agent.
- *Class G.* A Class G transformation is **geometric reconfiguration of the state space**: a change of the generator that changes which states are accessible. Under it, the system reorganizes onto the new corridor with its repertoire preserved or enlarged.

Both can pass through the same point in state space; they are opposite in what they do to dimensionality. So the decisive fact is not that description differs from control. It is that the *only* control that reproduces the trajectory does so by the dimensionality collapse the second horn names. The agency-preserving route reconfigures the generator, which no trajectory-forcing controller does. The impossibility claim is therefore not absolute over physics. It is exact over the conjunction that defines conversion: reaching the new corridor *while* keeping agency-bearing openness and the policy repertoire intact.

The four-way contrast makes the difference from each control strategy explicit:

| Type | Structure | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Damping | Reduces instability by general contraction toward a lower-energy state | Freezes agency; eliminates openness along with chaos |
| Feedback optimization | Corrects trajectory using the system's own dynamics | Collapses into self-salvation; no external gift (endogenous horn) |
| Coercive control | Imposes target state, bypassing orientation | Participation absent; identity overwritten (exogenous horn) |
| Class G | Contracts identity-destroying transverse modes while preserving agency-bearing tangential modes; externally sourced, receptivity-mediated | Requires both externality and receptivity; fails if either is absent |

Class G occupies a narrower formal space than any alternative: externally given, participatorily received, identity-preserving, and non-mechanical — which is to say, the space all three horns of the trilemma vacate.

### 8.2 Externality and Orientation-Gated Receptivity

The endogenous horn fails because change from $\langle F_0 \rangle$ is organization-preserving. The escape is *externality*: the transformative field must not lie in the span of the system's own dynamics. But pure externality, imposed without regard to the subject, is the exogenous horn. The escape from *that* is *receptivity*: the subject's orientation must modulate the coupling without generating the field.

In words, the transformative field is the product of two factors:
- an **external field**, not generated by the system's own dynamics;
- a **receptivity gate**, set by the subject's orientation.

The orientation does not create the field; it modulates receptivity to it. Each factor carries an exclusion:
- a field inside the span of the system's own dynamics returns the endogenous horn;
- a gate that does not vary with orientation returns coercion, because the outcome is then fixed whatever the subject's orientation.

A candidate transformation that fails either exclusion is not Class G. The formal statement is in Appendix A.5.

### 8.3 Definition of Class G as a Type Signature

A Class G intervention is an external, orientation-gated transformation of the effective landscape that lowers the transition barrier from dispersive dynamics toward Lambda-like persistence, while preserving agency, protecting identity-bearing modes, and preventing collapse into periodic closure. It is a type signature for admissible transformation, not a controllability theorem; it establishes no existence result and no constructive protocol.

| No. | Condition | Function | Horn it escapes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The barrier toward Lambda-like persistence is lower under the intervention than without it | Lowers the barrier toward transformation | — |
| 2 | Agency-bearing openness stays above zero | Preserves agency and exploration | Exogenous |
| 3 | Identity-bearing spread stays below its critical value | Protects identity-bearing invariants | — |
| 4 | Destructive transverse deviation contracts | Contracts destructive transverse deviation | — |
| 5 | The tangential rate is held near zero, or the tangential spectrum is otherwise protected | Prevents collapse into a point or loop | Exogenous |
| 6 | Long-time correlation power is nonzero | Preserves long-time memory | — |
| 7 | The identity-relevant observable has no exact period | Avoids mechanical periodicity | — |
| 8 | The drive is incommensurable or inexhaustible | Sustains aperiodicity (no fixed optimum target) | Optimization |
| 9 | The field factors into an external field and a receptivity gate that varies with orientation | Separates external gift from receptivity | Exogenous |
| 10 | The external field is not in the span of the system's own dynamics | Prevents reduction to endogenous feedback | Endogenous |

The conditions in symbols are in Appendix A.6.

The conditions are read as a single conjunctive exclusion criterion: each line rejects a class of near-miss transformations, and a candidate is G-admissible only if it survives all ten exclusions at once.

**The conjunction is satisfiable, and it discriminates. Both were checked, not assumed.**
- *Satisfiable.* A pre-registered test built an explicit stochastic system that meets all ten conditions at once, each against a threshold fixed in advance.
- *Discriminating.* Every near-miss in the filter table below fails the conditions the table predicts it should.
- *Not ten independent hurdles.* A second test found that about six of the ten can be broken in isolation, and the rest are entailed by others. The criterion is therefore a filter of roughly six independent constraints with several entailed consequences, and the count of ten should be discounted accordingly.

What this establishes is logical non-emptiness and exclusionary content, nothing more. Whether any biological, psychological or theological process instantiates the profile remains open. The measured values and the entailments are in Appendix A.6.

The conjunction may nonetheless be *empty* in many particular systems — and that is not a defect. If no intervention can contract destructive modes while preserving openness under realistic leakage and repair, the correct result is that no Class G transformation exists in that domain, and the grammar has done its work by identifying the absence rather than forcing a classification. (Structurally, conditions 1, 3, 4 make the identity-bearing region controlled-invariant in the sense of Nagumo and Aubin's viability theory, while conditions 2 and 5 forbid the trivial way of achieving invariance — collapse to a point; what distinguishes the present use from control engineering is only the indexing of the safe set to the contract *I* rather than to an engineering specification (Aubin 1991; Ames et al. 2019).)

## 9. The U-Limit and Class G: Identical Signature, Divergent Post-Jump Topology

The four-way comparison sorts *continuous, receptively mediated* transformations. It does not exhaust the externally sourced cases, because one important case is not a flow at all: a discrete unilateral jump in which the external transformation is not gated by the subject's antecedent orientation, yet does not annihilate the subject. Call this the U-limit. In the theological register, the U-limit is the occupant of unilateral originating transformation (regeneration), as Class G is the occupant of participatory transformation (sanctification).

### 9.1 The Admission of Initial Isomorphism

Earlier treatments tried to distinguish the U-limit from coercion by adjective — by calling one "integrity-bounded" and the other "violent" while their dynamics looked the same. This paper abandons that shielding and admits the isomorphism directly: *at the instant of transformation $t_0$, the U-limit and surgical coercion have the same dynamical signature.* Both are discrete unilateral jumps whose occurrence the antecedent orientation does not modulate. There is no feature of the jump *at $t_0$* that distinguishes a coercive override from a unilateral gift. Pretending otherwise is the error the present treatment corrects.

### 9.2 The Differentiation in Post-Jump Topology

The distinction is real but it lives *after* the jump, in the topology of the state space the subject is deposited into at $t_0^+$. This is where the U-limit and coercion diverge, and the divergence is categorical:

- *In coercion*, the jump deposits the system in a rigid fixed-point well — a Class P basin. The post-jump tangential spectrum is contracting; the agent's future is closed; agency is suppressed going forward. The jump produces a terminus.
- *In the U-limit*, the jump deposits the system on the adaptive, aperiodic central manifold of Class Lambda. The post-jump tangential spectrum is neutral and protected; a horizon of exploration and creative response opens; subsequent agency is *preserved*, $D_{ag}^{post} > 0$. The jump produces a new trajectory with an open future.

The two transformations are therefore indistinguishable at $t_0$ and opposite at $t_0^+$. What differentiates a unilateral gift from a unilateral violation is not the force of the jump but the *shape of the freedom it opens or forecloses*. This relocation of the distinction — from the instantaneous signature, where it cannot be drawn, to the post-jump topology, where it can — is the conceptual content of this section. It also explains why the U-limit is not "Class G intensified": driving the receptivity gate to an extreme within a Class G flow does not produce a discrete jump that opens a Lambda manifold. The two differ in logical type (flow versus jump) and the U-limit's distinctive mark is topological (where it lands), not parametric (how hard it pushes).

The synergetic theory of phase transitions in complex systems supplies a strictly formal analogy for what "opening a topology" means here, and it is worth stating precisely because it clarifies the geometry while incurring no mechanistic commitment. In Haken's account of non-equilibrium transitions, a system driven past a critical control value reorganizes around a newly emergent *order parameter* that enslaves the previously independent fast modes; the qualitative event is a discontinuous restructuring of the phase space — formally, a saddle-node or pitchfork bifurcation — in which the set of stable directions available to the system is reconfigured rather than merely shifted (Haken 1983). The analogy to the U-limit is exact at the level of geometry: a coercive jump corresponds to a bifurcation that collapses the post-transition phase space onto a single stable fixed point (the Class P well, all tangential directions contracting), whereas a U-limit corresponds to a bifurcation that opens a low-dimensional attracting manifold carrying neutral, non-contracting tangential directions (the Lambda manifold, $D_{ag}^{post} > 0$). The "horizon of exploration" that the U-limit opens is, in this picture, the family of marginal directions a post-bifurcation central manifold makes available — directions along which the system can move without being either restored to a point or dispersed.

Two guardrails keep this an analogy and nothing more. First, the order parameter is a *description* of the reorganized macroscopic dynamics, not an entity: the claim is emphatically not that the self *is* an order parameter, nor that regeneration *is* a bifurcation, only that the geometric structure "discontinuous reorganization of available stable directions" is shared between the formal object and the U-limit's post-jump topology. Second, the analogy is macroscopic and qualitative; it imports no claim about the microphysical realization of any such bifurcation in any substrate, which is exactly the kind of positive mechanistic commitment this paper's negative method declines to make. With those guardrails, the bifurcation picture earns its place: it makes vivid, in a vocabulary independent of the theological register, why a single discontinuous event can either close a future or open one, and why that difference is topological rather than a matter of the event's magnitude.

**An apparent contradiction that must be answered: how can post-jump topology be the locus of the distinction if *I* is non-measurable?** Section 2.2 demotes *I* to a pragmatic-indexing metamodel "not detectable in the flow," yet the present section places the entire U-limit/coercion distinction in the *post-jump topology* — whether the system lands on a Lambda manifold or in a Class P well — and the companion paper (Paper 2) builds an empirical protocol to detect exactly this. If the distinguishing feature is contract-relative and unmeasurable, how can a protocol measure it? The objection conflates two things the framework keeps strictly apart. *I* is non-measurable as a *verdict about identity*: no measurement of a flow can tell you, without a prior contract, whether the system that emerged "is the same person," because that question is constituted by the contract, not discovered in the dynamics. But the *geometric correlates* the contract indexes — the post-jump tangential spectrum, the agency-bearing diffusion $D_{ag}^{post}$, the presence or absence of a rigid fixed-point basin — are perfectly measurable *once a contract is fixed*. The logic is conditional, exactly as throughout: *given* a contract $I$ that makes (say) preserved adaptive openness constitutive, the claim "this jump deposited the system on a Lambda manifold rather than a P well" is an empirical claim about $D_{ag}^{post}$ and the post-jump spectrum, and it is testable. What is *not* testable is the contract's own correctness — whether preserved openness *ought* to count as constitutive of this identity — and that was never claimed to be. So there is no contradiction: the protocol of Paper 2 does not measure *I*; it measures the geometric quantities a *pre-specified* $I$ designates as load-bearing, and reports whether they are preserved through the transition. The non-measurability of *I* is the non-measurability of the *choice of what to hold constitutive*; the measurability of post-jump topology is the measurability of *whether the designated constitutive structure survived*. The distinction between a unilateral gift and a unilateral violation is therefore empirical relative to a contract and contestable across contracts — which is precisely the epistemic status the framework claims for every persistence judgment, not a special embarrassment of this section.

### 9.3 Admissibility Conditions for the U-Limit

The U-limit's conditions can be stated beside those of Class G, here in words (the formal predicate is in Appendix A.7). A unilateral intervention qualifies only if four conditions hold together:
- **Not endogenous.** It is not derivable from, or composable out of, the system's own dynamics. This is the endogenous horn applied to jumps.
- **Not gated.** The subject's orientation does not gate it.
- **Integrity-bounded.** Its magnitude stays below the integrity threshold ($\Delta_c$) set by the support capacity of the invariant, so that it transforms rather than shatters. This guards against Class R dissolution.
- **Opening.** It lands the trajectory in the viable region $\Omega_I$, the region in which the trajectory class is preserved, and it leaves agency open after the transition rather than closing it.

The last condition is the post-jump topology of §9.2: the U-limit opens a field of participation, where coercion closes one and annihilation leaves no "after" at all. The U-limit thus parallels Class G's exclusionary form while differing in logical type, and its distinctive condition is the topological one.

The status of $\Omega_I$ must be stated carefully, because a natural misreading would collapse the whole result. If $\Omega_I$ were a *fixed, antecedent* viability set — a region of state space delimited in advance by standing thermodynamic and flux constraints — then the U-limit would be no transcendental matter at all: a control theorist could model it as ordinary state-constrained optimization, writing a barrier functional whose cost diverges outside $\Omega_I$ and vanishes within it, so that the apparent axiological discontinuity $J \to J'$ reduces to a local reparameterization under a *higher invariant criterion* — survival within the antecedent envelope. The third horn would be neutralized: the system's meta-objective never changed; it was always viability within a pre-given $\Omega_I$, and conversion becomes constrained optimization. This reading must be refused, and refusing it fixes what $\Omega_I$ is. The viability region is *not* an antecedent set the jump must find; it is a manifold the reconfiguration *installs*. Two layers must be distinguished to avoid circularity. The thinnest layer — bare thermodynamic non-dissolution, the requirement that the post-jump state not be Class R disintegration — is indeed antecedent and generic, but it is far too weak to define $\Omega_I$; it merely excludes shattering, and a barrier functional enforcing only it would not pick out a Lambda-like corridor from the vast set of non-shattered states. The load-bearing layer — *which* corridor counts as viable-for-this-identity, the content that makes $\Omega_I$ the specific region it is — is fixed only *downstream* of the reconfiguration, because it is the new generator that determines which states are mutually accessible and therefore which region is a coherent persistence corridor at all. There is no $\Omega_I$ to optimize toward before the jump, because the accessibility structure that individuates $\Omega_I$ does not exist until the generator is reconfigured. This is exactly what breaks the duality with state-constrained optimization: state-constrained optimization presupposes the constraint set as given data of the problem, whereas here the constraint set is *a product of the transformation*, not an input to it. A barrier functional can penalize departure from a region only if the region is specified in advance; $\Omega_I$ is not, and cannot be, because specifying it requires the very generator the U-limit installs. The U-limit does not solve a constrained optimization over a pre-given viable set; it changes which optimization problem there is, and the viability set is an output of that change, not a boundary condition on it.

One further refinement protects the continuity axiom at the moment of deepest crisis, and it must be stated so as not to be confused with a failure mode this paper has already excluded. During the critical destructuring that precedes axiological reconfiguration, the projection operator of the pragmatic-indexing metamodel — the operator that reads the identity-relevant macro-variable off the underlying dynamics — does *not* collapse to the empty set ($\lim P \to \emptyset$). A projection going to zero would sever historical continuity outright, violate the non-branching constraint of section 3, and leave whatever emerged on the far side a *replacement* system rather than a transformation of the original; it would be Class R dissolution wearing the mask of conversion. Instead the operator contracts only to a critical residual floor, a *minimal continuity kernel* $P_\epsilon$. Under this regime of high informational vulnerability the longitudinal (identity-bearing) variety thins but does not rupture: enough of the historically indexed trajectory is carried across the crisis to keep the identity contract $I$ intact, while the orthogonal subspace $Q$ expands to admit the reconfiguration. This must not be read as the precision-collapse failure that elsewhere defines dispersal: $P_\epsilon$ is a *thinning of the projection channel under a strict positive floor*, not a flattening of the system's precision landscape to zero. The distinction is exact and load-bearing. A genuine collapse of precision across the board ($\Pi \to 0$) would disperse the system into Class M — motion without retention, the precise opposite of preserved identity — and is excluded for the same reason an empty projection is. What $P_\epsilon$ names is the complementary case: the projection narrows to its minimal historically continuous core precisely *so that* identity is not lost while the value structure is reordered around it. The floor $\epsilon > 0$ is what forbids both catastrophes at once — it is bounded away from zero (so the trajectory is never severed, against $P\to\emptyset$) and it is a property of the *projection*, not of a global precision parameter (so it carries no commitment to $\Pi\to 0$). In the language of section 9.3's integrity bound, $P_\epsilon$ is the projection-side counterpart of the magnitude bound $\Delta_c$: where $\Delta_c$ forbids a jump so large it shatters the invariant, $P_\epsilon$ forbids a projective thinning so complete it severs the invariant's history. Both are floors against dissolution, stated on the two faces — magnitude and projection — of the same continuity requirement.

## 10. Conclusion: The Insufficiency of the Control Paradigm

This paper has defended a single negative thesis as severely as it can. Persistence under transformation is neither frozen sameness nor unrestricted flux; it is identity-bearing continuity through change, and its transition problem — how a subject moves toward healthy persistence — is the point at which the dominant scientific vocabulary breaks down. The Trilemma of Cybernetic Impossibility argues that the three control-theoretic strategies for directed transition each fail when applied to a subject whose identity and agency must both survive: endogenous change is organization-preserving and hence not conversion (Ashby); exogenous control achieves the target only by driving agency to zero (annihilation); and optimization is undefined across the axiological discontinuity that conversion essentially is (Bellman collapse). The three are jointly exhaustive of the paradigm, given the definition of control the paper states,, so the result is transcendental: the control framework cannot *formulate* conversion, not merely cannot solve it.

What survives the trilemma is not a void but a precisely shaped admissibility profile, Class G — externally sourced, receptivity-gated, mode-selective, value-reordering — stated as a type signature rather than a mechanism. Its theological applications — to regeneration, sanctification, sanctity and acedia — are developed in the companion theological paper (in preparation), where each mapping is stated as an analogical clarification and not as a derivation.

The framework does not settle identity, consciousness, or resurrection, and it does not prove grace. Its contribution is to clear ground: to show, with structural rather than empirical force, that classical control science lacks the instruments even to *state* the problem of non-coercive transformation, and to specify the shape any adequate account must have. The mathematics disciplines distinctions, costs the exogenous horn quantitatively, and — for autonomous preference dynamics — excludes conversion — by recurrence for invariant-measure-typical states and, for dissipative transients, by an interpretive argument; theological uses of the grammar are left to the companion paper. The center of the paper is therefore the negative result and its residue: not a claim that a miracle has been explained, but an argument — definitional at its top level (§7.6), quantitative at the exogenous horn, and part measure-theoretic, part interpretive in the endogenous case — that the most powerful scientific account of directed change cannot deliver the very thing it would be asked to explain, agentive, value-reordering, non-coercive transformation, without destroying it.

> Persistence under transformation is neither frozen sameness nor unrestricted flux. The control paradigm can damp it, force it, or optimize within it; it cannot transform it without erasing it. What transformation without erasure requires is exactly what the paradigm leaves empty.

## Appendix A. Formal Apparatus

The body states the argument in prose. This appendix collects the formal statements the body relies on, in the order the body uses them. It adds no claim the body does not make, and where a passage below says "this section" or "above", it refers to the body section named in its heading.

### A.1 The Correlation-Power Condition for Lambda (§5.4)

For a mean-zero observable $f$, define the autocorrelation under the flow $C_f(t) = \langle f, U_t f \rangle$, with $U_t$ the Koopman evolution, and the correlation-power average $P_f(T) = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T |C_f(t)|^2\,dt$. A system preserves the relevant pattern if $\liminf_{T\to\infty} P_f(T) > 0$. For finite applications this is replaced by a pre-specified finite-window estimate over the relevant timescale, validated against phase-randomized surrogates so that a windowed estimate inseparable from its surrogates does not license a Lambda attribution. The condition distinguishes Lambda from Class M: both may be deterministic, bounded, and non-periodic, but M loses correlation power while Lambda retains it. This tracks the spectral dichotomy: an observable with non-trivial projection onto the point spectrum of $U_t$ yields almost-periodic, power-retaining correlations (Lambda-like and P), whereas an observable in the absolutely continuous spectrum has $C_f(t)\to 0$ by Riemann–Lebesgue (Class M) (Walters 1982; Halmos 1956). The same separation has an information-theoretic reading: in computational mechanics, memory-preserving structure shows substantial excess entropy on the identity-relevant observable while remaining aperiodic — neither random nor merely repeated — and is quantified by the statistical complexity of the process's causal states (Crutchfield and Young 1989; Crutchfield 2012).

### A.2 The Identity/Agency Decomposition (§7.1, §8.1)

Let the subject's dynamics, near the identity manifold $M_\Lambda$, be decomposed into a transverse identity-bearing subspace $E_{id}$ (whose uncontrolled diffusion destroys identity) and a tangential agency-bearing subspace $E_{ag}$ (whose variation constitutes adaptive openness): $TM_\Lambda = E_{id} \oplus E_{ag}$. Conversion requires contraction of destructive transverse deviation, $\lambda_\perp < 0$, with *protected* tangential openness, $\lambda_\parallel = 0$ (neither frozen nor dispersed), and the diffusion conditions $D_{id} < D_c$, $D_{ag} > 0$. The trilemma is that no control strategy can establish this profile.

Formally, near $M_\Lambda$ decompose the tangential space $TM_\Lambda = E_{id}\oplus E_{ag}$. Mode-selective transformation requires $\lambda_\perp < 0$ (destructive transverse deviation contracted), $\lambda_\parallel = 0$ or a protected non-mixing tangential spectrum (agency neither frozen nor dispersed), $D_{id} < D_c$ (identity-bearing diffusion below threshold), and $D_{ag} > 0$ (agency-bearing diffusion preserved). The pair $D_{id} < D_c$, $D_{ag} > 0$ is the exact negation of the exogenous horn's signature $D_{ag}\to 0$: where totalizing control collapses agency to guarantee identity, mode-selective transformation protects both by acting only on the destructive subspace.

The distinction is technical, not literary, and must be mapped back onto the apparatus rather than left to the word "agency." Exogenous forcing of a prescribed trajectory is *coercive dimensionality reduction*: to hold the system on the commanded path the controller drives the tangential spectrum strongly contracting ($\lambda_\parallel \to -\infty$, hence $D_{ag} \to 0$) and collapses the policy repertoire ($H(\pi)$ falls), so the realized trajectory is purchased by destroying the agentive degrees of freedom — the system reaches the geometry as a contracted point, not as an agent. A Class G transformation does the opposite at the level of the same variables: it is *geometric reconfiguration of the state space* — a change of the generator that re-metricizes which states are accessible — under which the system reorganizes onto the new corridor with its repertoire preserved or enlarged ($D_{ag} > 0$, $H(\pi)$ intact under the new metric). Both can pass through the same point in state space; they are opposite in what they do to dimensionality. Forcing reduces it by coercion; reconfiguration preserves it under a new geometry. This is why "describe is not control" was too weak as first stated: the decisive fact is not that description differs from control, but that the *only* control that reproduces the trajectory does so by the dimensionality collapse the second horn names, while the agency-preserving route is a reconfiguration of the generator that no trajectory-forcing controller performs. The impossibility claim is therefore not absolute over physics; it is exact over the conjunction that defines conversion — reaching the new corridor *while* holding $D_{ag} > 0$ and $H(\pi)$ intact — and that conjunction is precisely what trajectory forcing cannot satisfy.

### A.3 The Meta-Optimization Collapse Theorem (§7.5)

**The Meta-Optimization Collapse Theorem (endogenous-preference case).** Let the cost be parametrized by a value state $\theta$ that evolves under an endogenous dynamics $\dot\theta = f(\theta)$ (the agent revises its own preferences), and suppose the value state remains confined to a compact set $K$ — a necessary condition for the agent to be a persisting system rather than one whose values diverge to infinity. Decompose $f$ by Helmholtz–Hodge into a gradient part and a divergence-free part, $f = -\nabla V + g$ with $\nabla\!\cdot g = 0$ (Bhatia et al. 2013; Glötzl & Richters 2023). One technical caveat must be stated for the decomposition to be well-posed on a *compact* set $K$: on a bounded domain with boundary the Hodge decomposition acquires a third, harmonic component, $f = -\nabla V + g + h$ with $h$ both curl-free and divergence-free, and the decomposition is unique only once boundary conditions are fixed. The harmonic term does not open a fourth dynamical category: being curl-free it is locally the gradient of a harmonic potential and groups with Case 1 wherever it is exact, while any non-exact (cohomologically nontrivial) circulation it carries behaves dynamically like the divergence-free part and groups with Cases 2–3. The trichotomy below should therefore be read as a partition by *asymptotic behavior* (descent to a potential minimum; unbounded non-attracting drift; bounded attractor), which is exhaustive regardless of how the boundary-dependent harmonic remainder is apportioned. With that understood, exactly one of three cases holds, and *none* of the three is conversion.

*Case 1 — the integrable case (Bellman returns).* If $g\equiv 0$, or more generally if $f$ admits a global scalar potential (equivalently, an integrating factor rendering it a gradient flow), then $\dot\theta = -\nabla V$ is descent on a fixed meta-potential $V$. The "new" cost $J_{\theta(t)}$ is then not an axiological rupture but a moving coordinate within the single invariant objective $V$: the system is performing optimization in the extended space $(x,\theta)$, and the third horn of the trilemma — the Bellman incommensurability of §7.4 — applies to $V$ exactly as it applied to a fixed $J$. The meta-objective has simply absorbed the preference change. Conversion, which requires that there be *no* fixed evaluative point even at the meta-level, is excluded by hypothesis.

*Case 2 — the dispersive case (Class M).* If the divergence-free part dominates and the flow has no attracting invariant set within $K$ — equivalently, the value state wanders without settling — then preferences cycle: the divergence-free component of a value field generically induces intransitive preference loops ($A\succ B\succ C\succ A$, the classical "value pump"), and the agent suffers chronic temporal inconsistency. It never reaches a directed, permanent reordering; it fluctuates. This is precisely Class M dispersion in the companion vocabulary — motion without retention — and it fails conversion's first constitutive property, *permanent stabilization in a new regime*.

*Case 3 — the recurrent case (the third state, and the one a naive dichotomy misses).* The genuinely difficult case, and the reason this is a trichotomy and not the dichotomy it is sometimes mistaken for, is a flow with non-zero curl ($g\not\equiv0$, so it escapes Case 1) that *does* possess a bounded attracting invariant set in $K$ (so it escapes Case 2's unbounded dispersion). This regime subdivides into three attractor types, and conversion is excluded from each by two distinct arguments, which must be kept separate. (i) A *fixed-point* attractor (a damped spiral has non-zero curl yet settles to a point) reaches a permanent terminus but has zero asymptotic entropy: the agency-bearing openness $D_{ag}$ collapses at the fixed point, so it fails conversion's *second* property (non-coercive aperiodic openness) — and it is excluded by absence of openness, *not* by recurrence. (ii) A *limit cycle* has non-zero curl yet locks onto a permanent bounded set; it is rigidly periodic — closed recurrence, not an open new regime — and fails the first property. (iii) A *strange attractor* (e.g. a Lorenz flow) is non-gradient, bounded, and genuinely aperiodic with positive Lyapunov exponent — it satisfies conversion's second property but fails the first, and necessarily so. Here the recurrence argument applies, but with the measure stated correctly. A dissipative strange attractor does *not* preserve the ambient Lebesgue measure (it contracts phase-space volume); Poincaré recurrence cannot be invoked on the ambient space. It applies instead to the asymptotic dynamics restricted to the attractor itself, which carries a finite *invariant* (physical / SRB) measure: with respect to that measure the flow on the attractor is measure-preserving, and the Poincaré recurrence theorem (Poincaré 1890; Walters 1982) then guarantees that almost every point returns arbitrarily close to itself. Numerically, the Lorenz attractor revisits every neighborhood of its support under its SRB measure (recurrence fraction $\approx 1$) with net directed displacement a negligible fraction of its diameter; in direct computation this "$\approx 1$" holds for return radii $\varepsilon \gtrsim 0.05\cdot\text{span}$ (recurrence fraction $0.98$–$0.995$) and degrades to $\approx 0.51$ only at $\varepsilon = 0.01\cdot\text{span}$, so the claim is exact in the stated regime rather than for arbitrarily small neighborhoods. Aperiodic openness is therefore purchased *at the cost of recurrence on the attractor* — the system perpetually returns within its asymptotic set, the exact negation of "permanent reorganization into a new regime." The strange-attractor sub-case thus fails conversion's first property for a measure-theoretic reason: an autonomous flow whose asymptotic invariant measure is finite cannot be simultaneously open (positive entropy on the attractor) and non-recurrent (terminally directed) with respect to that measure.

Two limitations of the theorem are stated so that it is not over-read. First, it assumes the value state is *confined to a compact set*, so a preference dynamics permitted to grow without bound falls outside the theorem as stated and must be excluded separately. The natural thought — and the one an earlier version of this section relied on — is that unbounded value drift is itself a failure of persistence, the agent's values simply diverging, making it a more extreme Class M. That step carries more weight than the rest of the theorem, and direct testing shows it is **false**, though the conclusion it was meant to support survives under a better argument. Unbounded drift does not forfeit persistence: a quasi-periodic dynamics with incommensurable frequencies and an exponentially growing amplitude escapes every compact set while retaining correlation power far above the threshold this paper uses elsewhere ($P_f \approx 0.17$–$0.21$ against a $0.05$ bar, separated from phase-randomized surrogates at $p = 0.005$), and it does so while remaining aperiodic and non-chaotic — which is precisely the openness signature §8.3 demands of Class G. Calling such a system "a more extreme Class M" is simply inaccurate. What excludes it is not persistence but **recurrence on the quotient**: because an identity contract is indifferent to overall scale, it reads the direction, the direction is confined to a sphere, and the recurrence theorem the trichotomy already relies on applies there unchanged. The escaping cell is therefore closed by the *same* argument as the bounded cell rather than by a separate appeal to divergence, and the theorem is stronger for it. This closure does *not* extend automatically to every identity contract, and that limit is the theorem's real boundary rather than a technicality. What the recurrence argument requires is that the contract factor through a *bounded* reading: the direction on the sphere is one such reading, but so is any cardinal contract whose magnitude-dependence saturates. Within that class the closure does not depend on the contract being scale-free, which a direct test confirms rather than assumes. A family of *cardinal* contracts — ones treating the absolute magnitude of a value as constitutive by weighting the identity observable by that magnitude, from a bounded (saturating) reading through a linear one to the exponential $e^{\log r}$ — was applied to the same escaping witness. The bounded and linear readings leave the observable *recurrent* (measured return fractions $0.81$–$0.93$), because no magnitude weighting of that kind removes the return the oscillating direction on the quotient supplies, so the closure survives the move from scale-free to cardinal contracts. The exponential reading is a different matter, and recording it as a confirmed case would repeat precisely the error the companion paper is most severe about. Under that contract the measured correlation power collapses ($P_f = 0.0006$) while the bounded and linear readings of the identical system retain $P_f = 0.15$–$0.21$, and an earlier version of this section set the collapse aside as a numerical artefact of an $e^{25}$ observable. The structure of the exclusion forbids that inference. Contracts that grow fast are simultaneously the only ones with any prospect of breaking recurrence *and* the only ones that overflow the instrument, so the exclusion is **systematic and correlated with the property under test** — formally the same defect the companion paper's own fidelity analysis identifies, where a noisy proxy of a nonlinearly acting generator false-positives at a rate that grows with record length rather than behaving as sampling noise. The honest statement is therefore not that the cardinal family confirms recurrence, but that **exponential contracts are not testable by this instrument**. The attempted repair is itself instructive: measuring return in log coordinates, or with a scale-invariant statistic, converts the exponential contract into a linear one — which suggests that "does this unbounded observable return?" may be ill-posed absent a choice of scale, and that the choice of scale is itself the choice of contract.

### A.4 In-Silico Validation: Numbers and Arbitration (§7.7)

Two claims survived their strongest available tests. The **finite-gain agency cost** of §7.3 — asserted there to be graded and monotone rather than a step discontinuity — was checked on a double-well explorer held to a reference by proportional gain $k$: the agency-bearing diffusion falls $D_{ag}: 0.85 \to 0.012$ and the tangential relaxation rate drops $\lambda_\parallel: -0.09 \to -11.5$ as $k$ rises, both smooth and monotone, with the bifurcation at $k=1$ washed out by exploratory noise. The perfect-tracking annihilation limit is thus approached continuously, exactly as §7.3 claims, rather than being an artifact of the limit. The **trichotomy** of §7.5 was tested against its most-cited counterexamples classified by Lyapunov exponent, Poincaré recurrence, and the Helmholtz–Hodge split: gradient flow and curiosity land in Case 1 ($\lambda<0$, recurrence $>0.99$), a Hamiltonian flow in Case 3 ($\lambda\approx 0$, purely rotational), novelty search on a compact torus is bounded-recurrent rather than sustained-novel, and Lorenz chaos is the one positive-entropy case ($\lambda=+0.88$) yet remains recurrent ($0.995$). None instantiates the forbidden object — positive entropy together with absence of recurrence on a compact set — and sustained novelty without return required, in every case, leaving compactness (the escape horn), precisely as the theorem predicts. Leaving compactness, however, is *not* something that must be done to a system from outside, and a follow-up says so against the natural reading of this sentence: an autonomous field with no external or time-dependent term whatever — $\dot\theta = \theta\lVert\theta\rVert$ — departs every compact set in finite time. What that escape does *not* buy is non-return, and the pattern across the constructions tested is sharper than a blanket claim. Among those that are also *open* — radial escape carrying a chaotic direction, escape that endogenously brakes its own angular motion, and quasi-periodic escape with incommensurable frequencies — the identity-relevant observable remains **recurrent** without exception (measured return fractions $0.81$–$1.00$). The one escaping construction that does achieve non-return, a pure radial blow-up read under a cardinal contract (return fraction $0.00$), purchases it by freezing its direction entirely, and so fails the openness requirement instead: it is excluded by the other horn rather than by recurrence. Across the constructions tested, non-return and openness are available separately and not together — which is a statement about those constructions, not a demonstration that no construction could supply both. The reason is structural rather than incidental *for contracts that read the direction*: the escape occurs in the radial coordinate, while such a contract reads the *direction*, and the direction lives on a sphere — a compact quotient — where the recurrence theorem applies exactly as it does in the bounded case. Escape in a coordinate the contract cannot see purchases nothing. The force and the limit of that reason should be taken together: it holds for any contract factoring through a bounded reading, and it is silent about contracts that do not, whose decisive case the instrument cannot reach (§7.5). The disjunction is therefore closed on one branch and sampled on the other.

The third result requires the section to concede a genuine limit rather than claim a clean win, and it is the more important for the paper's honesty. The trichotomy was pushed into the regime where it is actually contestable: a six-dimensional, population-based agent that *mutates its own value base* — the closest in-silico analogue to the value-reordering the paper cares about. Among the cells that a resolution-converged integrator can resolve, the trichotomy holds without exception (four of twelve cells, all Case 1, $\lambda \approx -5$ to $-23$, high recurrence; no falsifier). But the eight strong-recession cells — those in which a fleeing value-bump passes *through* an agent, producing a near-discontinuous reconfiguration of the value landscape — are **resolution-divergent**: their apparent positive Lyapunov exponent grows as the integrator step shrinks ($\lambda = 121 \to 217 \to 528 \to 722$ as $dt \to dt/8$). A per-step ($1/dt$) blow-up of this kind is the classical signature of a *numerical artifact* rather than a genuine exponent, and on that reading none of these cells is a falsifier. The honest statement, however, is that the reading is **presumptive, not conclusive**: the scaling $\lambda\cdot dt = [2.43, 2.17, 2.64, 1.81]$ is bounded but noisy and non-monotone (not the clean constant a pure artifact gives), and — more substantively — the near-discontinuity that generates it *may be a genuine feature of abrupt value-base mutation*, not an integration pathology. A value dynamics that reconfigures near-discontinuously is exactly the phenomenon a formal theory of metanoia would most want to capture, and for such a dynamics the smooth-flow premise underwriting the Poincaré-recurrence argument does not straightforwardly hold. The one low-recurrence cell that could constitute a falsifier (recurrence $0.34$) is one of these unresolved cells, so its status is undecided by the E2 run itself and is resolved only by the arbitration that follows.

That follow-up resolves the regime, and it does so on a principle that makes the test decisive rather than suggestive: for a *Lipschitz* vector field on a compact set the finite-time Lyapunov exponent is finite and any error-controlled integrator must converge to it, so non-convergence as the integration error shrinks is a signature of genuine non-smoothness — the question "artifact or real discontinuity" is exactly "is the field genuinely non-Lipschitz, and if so, is the non-smoothness intrinsic to the value dynamics or an incidental coordinate seam?" A controlled decomposition answers it. First, under the original run's own Lyapunov bookkeeping — which renormalizes only the agent coordinates and leaves the co-evolving value-landscape perturbation uncontrolled — the blow-up *reproduces* ($\lambda = 217 \to 1360$ as the step is refined), so the dramatic divergence was dominated by that partial renormalization, whose uncontrolled component leaves the linear regime. Second, a proper full-state renormalization on the *same* field removes the blow-up but is still non-convergent and noisy, the residue of a genuine $C^0$ discontinuity — but that discontinuity is the *minimal-image seam* of the torus (a jump in the coordinate chart at the half-period cut locus, local Lipschitz constant $\approx 700$), an artifact of how distance was coordinatised, **not** an abrupt reordering of the objective. Replacing that seam with a genuinely smooth ($C^\infty$) periodic interaction kernel modelling the *same* population-based value-mutating dynamics — and using the full-state estimator — makes the exponent **converge in all eight previously-divergent cells** (local Lipschitz constant $\approx 32$), a convergence independently confirmed by an adaptive error-controlled solver whose estimate is flat at $\lambda \approx +0.09$ across six orders of magnitude in tolerance. The decisive low-recurrence cell converges to $\lambda \approx +0.06$ with recurrence $0.94$ — **not** the $0.34$ the minimal-image field reported — so *both* the Lyapunov divergence and the low recurrence were coordinate artifacts. Every previously-open cell is a genuine finite near-zero exponent with high recurrence: Case 3, the bounded-recurrent band the theorem allows (the same band Lorenz occupies at $\lambda = +0.88$, recurrence $0.995$), and no cell is the forbidden positive-entropy-without-recurrence object.

The theorem's scope can therefore be stated more strongly than the E2 run alone allowed, and without the earlier hedge: **the numerical classification holds for smooth autonomous value flows on a compact set across the full value-mutating sweep tested (these flows are dissipative, so their placement in Case 1 or Case 3 is a classification of where they settle, and excluding that settling as conversion remains the interpretive step of §7.5) — not merely the subset a fixed-step integrator happened to resolve — because the strong-recession divergence that appeared to threaten it was a coordinate-and-bookkeeping artifact, not a genuine dynamical discontinuity.** What that follow-up established is a *correction of instrument*, and it should not be inflated into more. In particular it does not lift the dimensionality delimitation of §1: a six-dimensional agent whose identity/agency mode decomposition is *supplied by its construction* sits, by this paper's own criterion, nearer the pole where a designer furnishes the decomposition than the high-dimensional, self-organizing regime in which that decomposition must be carved out by the system's own viability-constrained dynamics. Six dimensions is the hardest case this paper has *tested*, not the regime in which the impossibility is claimed to be exact, and the genuinely high-dimensional self-organizing regime remains untested here. One honest bound remains, and it is a matter of logic rather than a pending computation: this settles that *this* agent's apparent falsifier was numerical, and it cannot settle what a *genuinely* discontinuous value jump would do — but a genuine discontinuity is not a counterexample to a theorem about smooth flows. An abrupt, topology-changing reordering of the value base is precisely the object the paper cedes to Class G and the U-limit (§§8–9), where transformation is admitted as a change of the post-jump topology rather than a trajectory of the flow. The smooth-flow defense of §7.5/§7.6 is thus vindicated across the hardest case yet exposed to test, and the discontinuous case sits exactly where the paper always placed it — outside the flow, in the residual admissibility profile of Class G, not as a falsifier of the trichotomy.

### A.5 Externality and Orientation-Gated Receptivity (§8.2)

Let $W_G(x, t; \Xi, \theta) = \chi(\theta)\, W_{ext}(x, t; \Xi)$, where $W_{ext}$ is not generated by $F_0$, $\Xi$ is the external source, $\theta$ is orientation/receptivity, and $\chi(\theta)$ modulates coupling strength. The orientation does not create the field; it modulates receptivity to it. Each factor carries an exclusion. The requirement $W_{ext}\notin\langle F_0\rangle$ excludes endogenous optimization (the first horn). The requirement that $\chi(\theta)$ genuinely vary with $\theta$ excludes coercion (the second horn): if $\chi$ is constant in $\theta$, the outcome is fixed regardless of orientation, which is mechanical imposition. A candidate transformation failing either exclusion is not Class G — the first failure returns self-optimization, the second returns coercive control.

### A.6 The Class G Conditions in Symbols, and the Satisfiability Tests (§8.3)

| No. | Condition | Function | Horn it escapes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\Delta V^G_{M\to\Lambda} < \Delta V_{M\to\Lambda}$ | Lowers the barrier toward transformation | — |
| 2 | $D_{ag} > 0$ | Preserves agency and exploration | Exogenous |
| 3 | $D_{id} < D_c$ | Protects identity-bearing invariants | — |
| 4 | $\lambda_\perp < 0$ | Contracts destructive transverse deviation | — |
| 5 | $\lambda_\parallel = 0$ or protected tangential spectrum | Prevents collapse into a point or loop | Exogenous |
| 6 | Long-time correlation power nonzero | Preserves long-time memory | — |
| 7 | No $T>0$ with $f(\Phi_{t+T})=f(\Phi_t)$ for identity-relevant $f$ | Avoids mechanical periodicity | — |
| 8 | Incommensurable or inexhaustible drive | Sustains aperiodicity (no fixed optimum target) | Optimization |
| 9 | $W_G = \chi(\theta)W_{ext}$ | Separates external gift from receptivity | Exogenous |
| 10 | $W_{ext}\notin\langle F_0\rangle$ | Prevents reduction to endogenous feedback | Endogenous |

**The conjunction is satisfiable, and it discriminates — both were checked rather than assumed.** A conjunctive criterion of ten conditions invites two opposite and equally fatal objections: that it is *empty by over-specification* (no object whatever meets all ten, so the "residual profile" is a residue of nothing), or *vacuous by under-specification* (everything meets all ten, so the filter excludes nothing). A pre-registered test settles both. An explicit stochastic system — a regime coordinate on a double well, a contracted transverse deviation from an identity manifold, two protected phases advancing at incommensurable frequencies, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck agency coordinate, and an orientation variable driving a receptivity gate, with the intervention *lowering the landscape barrier* rather than commanding a path — satisfies all ten conditions simultaneously, each measured against a threshold fixed in advance: the barrier falls $0.250\to0.107$ (mean first-passage $386\to187$), agency variance is $0.50$, identity diffusion $0.020$ sits below a *measured* critical value $D_c=0.100$, $\lambda_\perp=-1.01$ while $\lambda_\parallel=0.000$, long-time correlation power is $0.22$, the identity observable admits no exact period (sup-norm period error $4\times10^{-2}$ against a $10^{-6}$ bar, on an instrument separately validated to flag genuinely periodic signals at $\approx10^{-8}$), the drive is incommensurable, the applied field factors exactly as $\chi(\theta)W_{ext}$ (rank-one to $10^{-16}$) through a gate that genuinely closes, and $W_{ext}$ resists projection onto a deliberately generous endogenous basis (residual $0.60$). So the ten conditions are mutually consistent. They also do real work: every near-miss in the filter table below is excluded, and each fails a condition the table predicts it should — classical damping on 2 and 5, ungated forcing on 9, endogenous feedback on 10, coercion on 2 and 9, a commensurable drive on 7 and 8. What this establishes is *logical* non-emptiness and exclusionary content, nothing more: whether any biological, psychological, or theological process instantiates the profile is exactly the question this section declines to answer, and remains open.

**The ten conditions are not ten independent hurdles, and the count overstates the filter's content.** A second test, prompted by the observation that three of the five near-misses above fail *more* conditions than the table predicts, measured how far the conditions are separable. Perturbing the witness to violate each condition in turn, and then measuring the whole battery, shows that **six of the ten** — conditions 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 9 — can be broken *in isolation*, by a perturbation that violates that condition and no other; the remaining four cannot. The measured entailments are $5\Rightarrow6$, $5\Rightarrow7$, $8\Rightarrow7$ and $10\Rightarrow1$, so the filter's **effective dimensionality is about six**. Which quantity that number reports is worth stating, since more than one is defensible and they do not agree: "six" counts conditions breakable *in isolation*, which is the most conservative of the available readings and the one least flattering to this section. Counted instead by *distinct failure signature* — how many of the ten behave differently under the battery of perturbations — the answer is nine, since only 7 and 8 coincide, and they do so inseparably; counted by distinct sets of perturbations that break each condition, it is ten. A reader recomputing this will therefore land somewhere between six and ten depending on the question asked, and the claim defended here is the lower bound. Two of those dependencies are matters of logic rather than artefacts of the particular witness, and would hold for any system: an incommensurable drive is aperiodic by definition, so condition 8 entails condition 7 and no construction can separate them; and collapsing the tangential spectrum (condition 5) necessarily destroys long-time correlation power (6) and renders the identity observable trivially periodic (7), since a collapsed phase is a constant. The remaining entailment ($10\Rightarrow1$: a field lying in the endogenous span does not lower the barrier) may be specific to this construction. None of this disturbs the satisfiability or discrimination results — the conjunction is still met, and every near-miss is still excluded — but the rhetorical force of "ten conditions" should be discounted accordingly: the criterion is a filter of roughly six independent constraints with several entailed consequences, and it is reported that way here rather than left for a reader to discover.

### A.7 The U-Limit Admissibility Predicate (§9.3)

The governing conditions can be stated beside those of Class G. For Class G, the diffusion constraints: $D_{id} < D_c$, $D_{ag} > 0$. For the U-limit, an integrity bound on the magnitude of the unilateral intervention together with a *post-transition* opening of agency:

$$U\text{-limit:}\qquad |W_U| < \Delta_c(Q_I, E), \qquad D_{ag}^{post} > 0,$$

where $W_U$ is the unilateral transformation, $\Delta_c(Q_I, E)$ is the integrity threshold set by the support capacity of the projected invariant and its boundary, and $D_{ag}^{post}$ is the agency-bearing diffusion available *after* the transition. The integrity bound requires that even an ungated jump remain below the magnitude that would *shatter* rather than transform the invariant — this is what keeps a U-limit from collapsing into the Class R dissolution that an unbounded jump would cause. The condition $D_{ag}^{post} > 0$ is the formal statement of the post-jump topology: the U-limit *opens* a field of participation, distinguishing it from coercion (which closes one) and from annihilation (which has no "post" at all).

A full admissibility predicate for the U-limit parallels the Class G conditions as a conjunction of exclusions:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{U-Adm}(x) \iff{} &\big[W_U \text{ functionally independent of } \mathcal{F}_{endo}\big] \;\land\; \big[\tfrac{\partial W_U}{\partial\theta}=0\big] \\ &\land\; \big[|W_U| < \Delta_c(Q_I, E)\big] \;\land\; \big[\mathbf{T}_U(x^-) \in \Omega_I\big], \end{aligned}$$

where $\mathcal{F}_{endo}$ is the set of transformations the system can actuate from its own resources, functional independence (a relation, stated in prose, not a geometric operator) means $W_U$ is not derivable, parameterizable, actionable, or explicable as a composition of the system's endogenous dynamics, and $\Omega_I$ is the viable region in which the trajectory class is preserved. The first conjunct excludes self-generated jumps (the endogenous horn applied to jumps); the second states orientation does not gate the transition; the third is integrity selectivity (against shattering); and the fourth requires the jump to land in the viable region — which, unpacked, is the requirement that the post-jump topology be Lambda-like rather than a Class P well or Class R dissolution. The U-limit thus parallels Class G's exclusionary form while differing in logical type, and its distinctive condition is the topological one.
