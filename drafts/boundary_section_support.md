# Support notes for the author: the system-boundary section of Paper 1

**This is working material, not paper text.** A reviewer of the development plan recommended that
the central argument of Paper 1 be written by the author: why relativity to the system boundary does
not trivialize the thesis. That section is where a referee will test whether the author commands the
thesis. These notes collect the problem, the options, the objections and the literature, so that the
author can write it. Nothing here should be pasted into the paper as it stands.

## 1. The problem, stated as a referee would state it

Paper 1 §6.5 now says the following. Relative to a declared system boundary, an autonomous value
dynamics does not realize conversion. A Class-G occasion is one whose source the declared boundary
excludes.

The objection writes itself. *Bring the trigger's source inside the boundary and the enlarged flow
is autonomous again. Its new regime is then an attractor latent in that flow, and by the paper's
own argument the change is reabsorption. So any Class-G event can be reclassified as reabsorption
by redrawing the boundary. If the verdict depends only on where the observer draws the line, the
thesis says nothing about the agent.*

At present the paper *concedes* the relativity in one paragraph. It does not argue that the
relativity leaves a substantive thesis standing. That argument is the missing section.

## 2. The three exits (from the reviewer's plan), with an assessment of each

**(a) Accept the relativity and tie it to the pragmatic contract I (§2.2).**
- The thesis becomes: *relative to an individuation of the agent, conversion requires an external
  occasion.*
- This is coherent with the paper as it stands. §2.2 already makes identity a contract declared by
  an observer, fixed in advance and contestable by rivals.
- What the section must show is that the relativity is **not arbitrary** in the way the objection
  needs.
  - The boundary is declared *before* the analysis, under the same three locks as *I* (§4.1).
  - Once declared, the trichotomy delivers real exclusions *for that agent*.
- The objection's move is to redraw the boundary *after* seeing the event. That is exactly the
  post-hoc contract selection §2.2 rules out.
- *Assessment:* the only exit defensible on a short timescale, and the one the paper already
  gestures at.

**(b) Propose a non-arbitrary boundary criterion.** Two candidates:
- Markov blankets (Kirchhoff et al. 2018), which are criticized as not picking out a unique
  boundary (Bruineberg et al. 2021).
- Organizational closure or autopoiesis (Maturana and Varela 1980; Di Paolo 2005 on adaptivity).

*Assessment:* stronger if it works, but it imports a contested metaphysics of individuation. The
Markov-blanket literature itself disputes whether blankets are objective features or modelling
choices, and that dispute is the same boundary problem one level down.

**(c) Show that every choice of boundary reproduces the trichotomy at some level.**
- The strongest result, and also the hardest.
- It would need an argument that nesting boundaries preserves the cell structure.
- Such an argument risks collapsing into "at the largest boundary everything is autonomous". That
  makes conversion impossible *tout court* and contradicts Class G's non-emptiness.

*Assessment:* not recommended without new mathematics.

## 3. Arguments the author might use under exit (a)

These are candidate arguments, stated as prompts. They are not settled.

1. **Frame-relativity is not triviality.**
   - Velocity is frame-relative, yet "this body is accelerating in that frame" is a substantive
     claim.
   - The question is whether the verdict is *invariant under admissible redescriptions*: those
     that keep the same individuation of the agent.
   - Under such redescriptions the trichotomy's verdict does not change. Only a change of *which
     agent* is being assessed changes it.
2. **Redrawing the boundary changes the subject of the claim.**
   - When the trigger's source is included, the system assessed is no longer the agent. It is the
     agent plus its occasion.
   - "The agent-plus-occasion did not convert" is compatible with "the agent was converted".
   - The objection establishes the first claim, not the negation of the second.
3. **The contract I already carries this structure.**
   - §2.2 accepts that "same?" means "same *what*?". The boundary question asks "converted by
     *what*, relative to *what*?".
   - The section could make explicit that the boundary is a component of the contract: *I* fixes
     both the invariants and the individuation.
4. **What would count against the thesis under (a).**
   - A declared boundary, fixed in advance, inside which an autonomous flow shows directed,
     permanent, agency-preserving reorganization of its top-level evaluative structure.
   - Stating this keeps the thesis falsifiable relative to the declaration.

## 4. What the section must concede

- Under (a) the thesis is **individuation-relative**. A reader with a different theory of agent
  individuation may reject its application to a given case.
- The theological reading (now in `drafts/theological_companion.md`) is **one declared boundary
  among others**, and the argument does not establish it.
- For dissipative dynamics, the exclusion still rests on the reabsorption interpretation, not on a
  theorem (§6.5).

## 7. A colleague's observations (2026-09-26), with an assessment

These come from the Paper 1 development plan (`drafts/paper1_restructure_plan.md`, phase 2.1).
All three are right. The last carries a caveat.

1. **Declaring the boundary in advance does not answer arbitrariness.**
   - The argument in §2 above says that redrawing the boundary *after* seeing the event is
     post-hoc selection, which §2.2 already forbids. That is true, and it is not enough.
   - An observer can declare a narrow boundary *in advance*, precisely so that the event will
     come out as Class G. The rule against post-hoc selection stops cheating after the fact. It
     does not stop a tendentious choice made before it.
   - The section needs a restriction on *which* boundaries are admissible, not only on *when*
     they are declared.
2. **A candidate restriction from within the philosophy of action.**
   - Individuate the agent by the boundary used to assign responsibility or practical identity:
     what the agent can answer for as its own. The obvious sources are Korsgaard on practical
     identity (*The Sources of Normativity*, 1996, lecture 3) and Frankfurt on identification
     ("Identification and Wholeheartedness", 1988). Both are verified in
     `references/paper1_phase3_literature.md`.
   - This sits between exits (a) and (b). It is not pure relativity, and it is not a
     Markov-blanket metaphysics, because it uses a criterion the field already accepts.
   - **Caveat.** Practical identity and identification are *contents*, and conversion may
     change exactly those contents. A boundary drawn by what the agent identifies with could
     move with the very event it is meant to classify. That reproduces the problem.
   - A criterion that survives conversion is the *formal* accountability relation, not its
     content: the responsibility-bearing, non-branching history that §4.3 already treats as a
     constitutive invariant of moral conversion. The agent is whoever can be held to answer,
     before and after, for what was done under the old values. Whether this is better than
     Korsgaard's or Frankfurt's criterion is the author's call. It has the advantage of already
     being in the paper.
3. **The velocity analogy works only if the invariant is named.**
   - Velocity depends on the frame; acceleration is invariant across inertial frames. So the
     analogy needs to say what plays the part of acceleration here: the verdict, *given* a fixed
     individuation of the agent. That is §3's first argument, and the section should say it in
     one sentence.
   - On the choice among the four arguments of §3, the colleague judges argument 2 ("redrawing
     the boundary changes the subject of the claim") the strongest, because it defuses the
     objection without conceding anything. I agree.
   - Argument 2 works only together with the admissibility restriction of point 1. Without it,
     "which subject?" has no principled answer, and the objection returns as "why this
     subject?".
