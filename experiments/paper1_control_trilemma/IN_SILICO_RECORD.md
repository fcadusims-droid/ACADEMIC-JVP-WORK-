## In-silico validation: numbers and arbitration

Two claims survived their strongest available tests. The **finite-gain agency cost** of §7.3 — asserted there to be graded and monotone rather than a step discontinuity — was checked on a double-well explorer held to a reference by proportional gain $k$: the agency-bearing diffusion falls $D_{ag}: 0.85 \to 0.012$ and the tangential relaxation rate drops $\lambda_\parallel: -0.09 \to -11.5$ as $k$ rises, both smooth and monotone, with the bifurcation at $k=1$ washed out by exploratory noise. The perfect-tracking annihilation limit is thus approached continuously, exactly as §7.3 claims, rather than being an artifact of the limit. The **trichotomy** of §7.5 was tested against its most-cited counterexamples classified by Lyapunov exponent, Poincaré recurrence, and the Helmholtz–Hodge split: gradient flow and curiosity land in Case 1 ($\lambda<0$, recurrence $>0.99$), a Hamiltonian flow in Case 3 ($\lambda\approx 0$, purely rotational), novelty search on a compact torus is bounded-recurrent rather than sustained-novel, and Lorenz chaos is the one positive-entropy case ($\lambda=+0.88$) yet remains recurrent ($0.995$). None instantiates the forbidden object — positive entropy together with absence of recurrence on a compact set — and sustained novelty without return required, in every case, leaving compactness (the escape horn), precisely as the theorem predicts. Leaving compactness, however, is *not* something that must be done to a system from outside, and a follow-up says so against the natural reading of this sentence: an autonomous field with no external or time-dependent term whatever — $\dot\theta = \theta\lVert\theta\rVert$ — departs every compact set in finite time. What that escape does *not* buy is non-return, and the pattern across the constructions tested is sharper than a blanket claim. Among those that are also *open* — radial escape carrying a chaotic direction, escape that endogenously brakes its own angular motion, and quasi-periodic escape with incommensurable frequencies — the identity-relevant observable remains **recurrent** without exception (measured return fractions $0.81$–$1.00$). The one escaping construction that does achieve non-return, a pure radial blow-up read under a cardinal contract (return fraction $0.00$), purchases it by freezing its direction entirely, and so fails the openness requirement instead: it is excluded by the other horn rather than by recurrence. Across the constructions tested, non-return and openness are available separately and not together — which is a statement about those constructions, not a demonstration that no construction could supply both. The reason is structural rather than incidental *for contracts that read the direction*: the escape occurs in the radial coordinate, while such a contract reads the *direction*, and the direction lives on a sphere — a compact quotient — where the recurrence theorem applies exactly as it does in the bounded case. Escape in a coordinate the contract cannot see purchases nothing. The force and the limit of that reason should be taken together: it holds for any contract factoring through a bounded reading, and it is silent about contracts that do not, whose decisive case the instrument cannot reach (§7.5). The disjunction is therefore closed on one branch and sampled on the other.

The third result requires the section to concede a genuine limit rather than claim a clean win, and it is the more important for the paper's honesty. The trichotomy was pushed into the regime where it is actually contestable: a six-dimensional, population-based agent that *mutates its own value base* — the closest in-silico analogue to the value-reordering the paper cares about. Among the cells that a resolution-converged integrator can resolve, the trichotomy holds without exception (four of twelve cells, all Case 1, $\lambda \approx -5$ to $-23$, high recurrence; no falsifier). But the eight strong-recession cells — those in which a fleeing value-bump passes *through* an agent, producing a near-discontinuous reconfiguration of the value landscape — are **resolution-divergent**: their apparent positive Lyapunov exponent grows as the integrator step shrinks ($\lambda = 121 \to 217 \to 528 \to 722$ as $dt \to dt/8$). A per-step ($1/dt$) blow-up of this kind is the classical signature of a *numerical artifact* rather than a genuine exponent, and on that reading none of these cells is a falsifier. The honest statement, however, is that the reading is **presumptive, not conclusive**: the scaling $\lambda\cdot dt = [2.43, 2.17, 2.64, 1.81]$ is bounded but noisy and non-monotone (not the clean constant a pure artifact gives), and — more substantively — the near-discontinuity that generates it *may be a genuine feature of abrupt value-base mutation*, not an integration pathology. A value dynamics that reconfigures near-discontinuously is exactly the phenomenon a formal theory of metanoia would most want to capture, and for such a dynamics the smooth-flow premise underwriting the Poincaré-recurrence argument does not straightforwardly hold. The one low-recurrence cell that could constitute a falsifier (recurrence $0.34$) is one of these unresolved cells, so its status is undecided by the E2 run itself and is resolved only by the arbitration that follows.

That follow-up resolves the regime, and it does so on a principle that makes the test decisive rather than suggestive: for a *Lipschitz* vector field on a compact set the finite-time Lyapunov exponent is finite and any error-controlled integrator must converge to it, so non-convergence as the integration error shrinks is a signature of genuine non-smoothness — the question "artifact or real discontinuity" is exactly "is the field genuinely non-Lipschitz, and if so, is the non-smoothness intrinsic to the value dynamics or an incidental coordinate seam?" A controlled decomposition answers it. First, under the original run's own Lyapunov bookkeeping — which renormalizes only the agent coordinates and leaves the co-evolving value-landscape perturbation uncontrolled — the blow-up *reproduces* ($\lambda = 217 \to 1360$ as the step is refined), so the dramatic divergence was dominated by that partial renormalization, whose uncontrolled component leaves the linear regime. Second, a proper full-state renormalization on the *same* field removes the blow-up but is still non-convergent and noisy, the residue of a genuine $C^0$ discontinuity — but that discontinuity is the *minimal-image seam* of the torus (a jump in the coordinate chart at the half-period cut locus, local Lipschitz constant $\approx 700$), an artifact of how distance was coordinatised, **not** an abrupt reordering of the objective. Replacing that seam with a genuinely smooth ($C^\infty$) periodic interaction kernel modelling the *same* population-based value-mutating dynamics — and using the full-state estimator — makes the exponent **converge in all eight previously-divergent cells** (local Lipschitz constant $\approx 32$), a convergence independently confirmed by an adaptive error-controlled solver whose estimate is flat at $\lambda \approx +0.09$ across six orders of magnitude in tolerance. The decisive low-recurrence cell converges to $\lambda \approx +0.06$ with recurrence $0.94$ — **not** the $0.34$ the minimal-image field reported — so *both* the Lyapunov divergence and the low recurrence were coordinate artifacts. Every previously-open cell is a genuine finite near-zero exponent with high recurrence: Case 3, the bounded-recurrent band the theorem allows (the same band Lorenz occupies at $\lambda = +0.88$, recurrence $0.995$), and no cell is the forbidden positive-entropy-without-recurrence object.

The theorem's scope can therefore be stated more strongly than the E2 run alone allowed, and without the earlier hedge: **the numerical classification holds for smooth autonomous value flows on a compact set across the full value-mutating sweep tested (these flows are dissipative, so their placement in Case 1 or Case 3 is a classification of where they settle, and excluding that settling as conversion remains the interpretive step of §7.5) — not merely the subset a fixed-step integrator happened to resolve — because the strong-recession divergence that appeared to threaten it was a coordinate-and-bookkeeping artifact, not a genuine dynamical discontinuity.** What that follow-up established is a *correction of instrument*, and it should not be inflated into more. In particular it does not lift the dimensionality delimitation stated at the outset (the second of the two delimitations): a six-dimensional agent whose identity/agency mode decomposition is *supplied by its construction* sits, by this paper's own criterion, nearer the pole where a designer furnishes the decomposition than the high-dimensional, self-organizing regime in which that decomposition must be carved out by the system's own viability-constrained dynamics. Six dimensions is the hardest case this paper has *tested*, not the regime in which the impossibility is claimed to be exact, and the genuinely high-dimensional self-organizing regime remains untested here. One honest bound remains, and it is a matter of logic rather than a pending computation: this settles that *this* agent's apparent falsifier was numerical, and it cannot settle what a *genuinely* discontinuous value jump would do — but a genuine discontinuity is not a counterexample to a theorem about smooth flows. An abrupt, topology-changing reordering of the value base is precisely the object the paper cedes to Class G and the U-limit (§§8–9), where transformation is admitted as a change of the post-jump topology rather than a trajectory of the flow. The smooth-flow defense of §7.5/§7.6 is thus vindicated across the hardest case yet exposed to test, and the discontinuous case sits exactly where the paper always placed it — outside the flow, in the residual admissibility profile of Class G, not as a falsifier of the trichotomy.
